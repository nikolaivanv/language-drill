## es-a1-gender-agreement
- consonant-final adjectives add -a → **fixed** (B&B §5.2.2). The rule is now limited to nationality adjectives and adjectives in -or/-ón/-án/-ín. An added sentence says most other consonant-final adjectives have one form for both genders (un libro fácil / una pregunta fácil; azul, joven, feliz).

## es-a1-noun-plural
- país explained as a vowel-final/stressed case → **fixed** (B&B §2.1.3). país now sits under Rule 2 (consonant → +es) in the rule text, the table row, the example note and the pitfall. The pitfall no longer says "ends in a vowel letter".
- "consonant → -es" stated as absolute → **fixed** (B&B §2.1.5a). Rule 2 now gives the exception for an unstressed vowel + -s (el lunes → los lunes, la crisis → las crisis), and the pitfall says "(other than unstressed -s, as in lunes)".
- stressed -í/-ú "add -es" as the only option → **fixed** (B&B §2.1.3c). Rule 3 is rewritten: everyday nouns add -s (menús, champús), and nationality words also have a formal -es plural (marroquíes, hindúes).

## es-a1-present-yo-go

  - "What is it?" now sorts the verbs into two groups: the pure yo-only verbs (hacer, poner, salir, traer, -zco) and the stem-changing ones (tienes, vienes, dices, oyes).

  - In the callout, "the -go ending lives only in yo" plus a note on the stem changes keeps its tengo/tienes example consistent.

## es-a1-relative-que-basic
- "the verb agrees with the antecedent" → **fixed**. The formation prose now says the verb agrees with its own subject: the antecedent when que is the subject (los chicos que viven), the real subject when que is the object (los libros que leo — yo). The callout is limited to the subject case.
- *la que vive marked ungrammatical → **fixed** (B&B §40.1.4). The ✗ forms are now the genuinely impossible *los chicos ques viven aquí / *las revistas ques leo. A note adds that la que vive aquí ("the one who lives here") is a different, correct structure.
