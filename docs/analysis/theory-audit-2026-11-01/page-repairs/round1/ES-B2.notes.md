# ES-B2 theory patch notes

Patch: `patches/ES-B2.json` (20 pages; all print `ok` with no mixed-script warnings). Verified against Butt & Benjamin where a section was cited.

## es-b2-appreciative-suffixes
- ie/ue "final syllable" + Latin American plain -ito (B&B §43.2.1(1)): **fixed**. The formation paragraph now says "first syllable" and marks the -ecito pattern as the usage of Spain, with *puertita/piedrita/florcita* as equally correct in Latin America. The table row for puerta now reads "ue in the first syllable" with "puertecita (Spain) / puertita (Lat. Am.)". The pitfall's trigger list is corrected.
- -cito only after polysyllables; monosyllables take -ecito (B&B §43.2.1(2)): **fixed**. The formation paragraph says "more than one syllable" and adds one-syllable words. A new table row was added: flor → florecita (Spain) / florcita (Lat. Am.). The pitfall's trigger list is updated.

## es-b2-causal-connectors
- Evidential *ya que* starred as unnatural: **fixed**. The when-to-use item now says *ya que / puesto que* also fill the justifying role, with *Debe de haber llovido, ya que el suelo está mojado* as the example. The false pitfall is replaced by a real one: dropping the comma, which turns enunciative *porque* into a causal reading. That pitfall now shows *Están en casa, ya que veo luz encendida* as correct.

## es-b2-cleft-sentences
- *que galicado* presented as ungrammatical (B&B §41.3 (a)–(b)): **fixed**. After a place, time or prepositional focus, plain *que* is now described as common Latin American usage, avoided in Spain and in formal writing. The asterisk is gone from *Es con ella que tienes que hablar*. After a person focus, *Fue Juan que llamó* stays non-standard, consistent with the curriculum fix and B&B. The example note now says "is not standard" instead of "incorrect".
- Tense matching stated as absolute, contradicted by *Es aquí donde nos conocimos*: **fixed**. "Usually agrees" in the template paragraph. The tense paragraph now says this is a tendency (*Es aquí / Fue aquí donde nos conocimos* are both fine). The example note no longer gives a bogus reason.

## es-b2-clitic-advanced
- Resumptive clitic obligatory only with definite/specific fronted objects (B&B §14.10.1): **fixed**. "Definite" is added in what-is-it, when-to-use, formation and the pitfall. The when-to-use item gives clitic-less fronting examples: *Carne no como; ¡Cuántas tonterías dices!; Eso creo yo*.

## es-b2-complex-conditionals
- Simple conditional for a present result banned (B&B §29.4 note 3): **fixed**. The pitfall is retitled "Using the simple conditional for a past result" and adds *Si hubieras estudiado más, ahora tendrías trabajo* as correct.
- *hubiera* + participle in the result clause not mentioned; the third pitfall was garbled (B&B §29.4, §29.6): **fixed**. What-is-it, the formation table and the hubiera/hubiese paragraph now give *hubiera* + participle as an equal alternative to *habría*. The third pitfall now targets the real restriction: the -se form *hubiese* does not replace *habría* in careful Spanish (B&B §29.6).

## es-b2-conditional-conjecture
- Self-contradictory callout on ser/tener stems: **fixed**. It now says *ser* is regular (*sería*), and *tener* and *haber* have irregular stems (*tendr-*, *habr-*), matching the table.

## es-b2-conditional-connectors
- *siempre que* + indicative = "whenever" (B&B §20.4.8, §20.5.6 n.1): **fixed**. What-is-it says "used as a condition, each one governs a set mood". The when-to-use item adds *La saludaba siempre que la veía*. The table cell for siempre que reads "subjunctive (+ indicative = «whenever»)".

## es-b2-correlative-comparison
- "always cuanto más": **fixed**. It is now the "safe, pan-Hispanic frame", and the pitfall notes that *mientras más* is common in Latin America and *entre más* is accepted in Mexico and Central America.
- *más y más* "does not exist": **fixed**. The asterisks are removed and it now reads "exists, but it is far less idiomatic". *cada vez más* stays the usual form (B&B §6.12).

## es-b2-nosotros-imperative
- The yo-form recipe fails for stem-changers: **fixed**. The formation paragraph adds that e→ie / o→ue does not carry into nosotros (*empiezo → empecemos, vuelvo → volvamos*), and that -ir stem-changers take e→i / o→u (*pidamos, durmamos*). The table row empiezo → empecemos is now consistent with the rule.
- *vayamos* starred as an affirmative command (B&B §21.5 n.1): **fixed** in the when-to-use item, the *Vámonos* example note and the warn callout. *Vamos* is "usual" and *vayamos* "also occurs, mainly in set phrases like *vayamos al grano*". The *no vamos* warning is kept.

## es-b2-passive-voice
- The pitfall example was not a se-passive: **fixed**. The callout is replaced with ✗ *Pisos se venden en esta calle.* / ✓ *Se venden pisos en esta calle.* This reuses the page's own example sentence.

## es-b2-past-subjunctive
- "-ara and -iera are two interchangeable forms; -ase regional": **fixed**. The what-is-it paragraph now gives the -ra set (-ara / -iera by conjugation) and the -se set (-ase / -iese). It says -se is used throughout the Spanish-speaking world, especially in Spain and in writing. This matches the formation callout.

## es-b2-perception-verbs
- *ver/oír que* "always a neutral alternative": **partially fixed**. B&B §24.7 n.2 does treat *oí que alguien entraba* as equivalent to *oí entrar a alguien*, and the curriculum fix skipped this finding, so I did not call the que-clause wrong. I removed "always available as a neutral alternative" and "always grammatically correct". I added that *oír que* / *ver que* can also mean "hear (be told) that" / "realize that". The example note no longer calls it "grammatically neutral".

## es-b2-quantifiers-advanced
- *que* "only before a clause or pronoun": **fixed**. Both the when-to-use item and the pitfall now say *que* introduces whatever is being compared against, including a noun phrase (*que su hermano*). They add the rule of thumb "de + quantity multiplied, que + comparison target".

## es-b2-remote-conditionals
- False "quick rule" callout (B&B §29.2, §29.4): **fixed**. The rule is rewritten: in a conditional, *si* never takes the conditional or the present subjunctive; it takes the imperfect subjunctive for remote conditions, indicative tenses (including past ones) for open conditions, and the pluperfect subjunctive for unfulfilled past conditions.
- Imperfect indicative after *si* "only in reported speech" (B&B §29.2(c),(e), n.1): **fixed**. The pitfall now gives the habitual case (*Si llovía, nos quedábamos en casa*) and the reported case (*Dijo que se quedaría en casa si llovía*). I replaced the old pluperfect example, which did not illustrate the imperfect.
- "The two tenses must match / cannot mix" (B&B §29.4 n.3): **fixed**. The formation text now calls this the core pairing and names both mixed-time types with examples.
- *ir → ir-*, *ser → ser-* listed as irregular stems: **fixed**. The list is now tendr-, podr-, har-, dir-, sabr-. It states that *ir* and *ser* are regular (*iría, sería*).
- Present subjunctive "used after si in indirect questions" (B&B §20.12.1): **fixed**. The aside now says the present subjunctive is never used after *si* in standard Spanish, in conditionals or in indirect questions (*no sé si viene*).

## es-b2-reported-speech-backshift
- *me pidió que la ayude* starred as "never" (B&B §20.8(e), n.4): **fixed**. The pitfall is now "Defaulting to the present subjunctive". The imperfect is the default, and the present is accepted while the request still applies (common in the media and in Latin America). The asterisk is gone, and the page's own example (a completed move) is explained as needing the imperfect.
- "*dijo que llegó* imprecise in writing": **skipped**. It is a defensible register simplification and the text is not starred. B&B §18.3.1 n.1 calls preterite-for-pluperfect colloquial ("especially in Latin America"). The curriculum fix made the same call.

## es-b2-sino-adversatives
- "Pero can only link two full clauses": **fixed**. The pitfall now says *pero* links adjectives and phrases too (*es pobre pero honrado*) but cannot correct a negated element. The formation table's "what follows" cell for *pero* is widened to "any clause, phrase or adjective", which keeps it consistent with *Habla francés, pero mal*.

## es-b2-subjunctive-compound
- "Do not use the pluperfect subjunctive when the main clause is in the present" (B&B §20.8): **fixed**. The sentence is replaced with "a tendency, not a rigid rule". It gives the counterfactual case (*No creo que lo hubiera conseguido sin ti*), the past-before-past case (*Me extraña que no lo hubieran avisado antes de la reunión*) and *ojalá*.

## es-b2-subjunctive-negated-opinion
- *no me dijo que había venido* starred (B&B §20.3.15 n.2): **fixed**. The pitfall is rewritten so the mood carries meaning: indicative when the fact is taken as true, subjunctive when the content is denied or doubted. The when-to-use item, the table row ("indicative if the content is a known fact") and the example note are updated to match.
- "whenever/always" ignores negative imperatives and questions (B&B §20.3.15 n.1, §20.3.16): **fixed**. The when-to-use lead-in now says negative commands and questions keep the indicative (*No creas que es fácil; ¿No es verdad que ha dicho eso?*). The *no es verdad que* pitfall is scoped to statements, with the question case noted.

## es-b2-subjunctive-temporal-concessive
- *por mucho/más que* "always subjunctive" (B&B §20.5.2): **fixed**. The when-to-use item now says subjunctive for future or hypothetical cases and indicative for real events (*Por más que se lo dije, no lo hizo.*). The example note no longer says "always requires".

## es-b2-verbs-of-change
- *llegar a ser* called reflexive/pronominal: **fixed**. What-is-it now says "six constructions… *llegar a ser* (the only one without a reflexive pronoun)". The formation text says five of the six are reflexive and that *llegar a ser* takes no pronoun (never **se llegó a ser*).
- *hacerse* "implies agency and intention" (B&B §31.3.3): **fixed**. The when-to-use item now says "often (though not always)" and adds *hacerse mayor, se hace tarde*. The pitfall is retitled "hacerse with loco and similar adjectives": *se hizo rico* is fine, but *loco* takes *volverse*. The **Se hizo loco* asterisk is kept.
