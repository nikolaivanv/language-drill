# Round 3 — ES verdicts

## es-a1-gender-agreement
- #1 **real**. "Only add -es in the plural" fits only -í words. Belga and canadiense are also gender-invariable gentilicios, and they add -s (B&B §5 gentilicio table: belga, canadiense, estadounidense). The intro now says they "change only in the plural (marroquí → marroquíes, belga → belgas, canadiense → canadienses)".

## es-a2-articles-use
- #1 **real**. B&B §4.1.9 says a qualified noun "may be" obligatorily specific, but a phrase that is still generic can omit the article (*soy profesor de español*; *tú eres (un) hombre respetable*; §4.1.3(2)). Changes:
  - when-to-use now reads "A phrase that only names the kind of job keeps zero article (Es profesora de inglés), but when an adjective or clause describes the person as an individual, the indefinite article usually returns".
  - The pitfall's "once a modifier is added, the article is required" became "once an adjective or clause describes her as an individual, the article is normally used".
  - The table header was left as is, because its example (Es un médico muy bueno) is correct.

## es-a2-cada-mismo
- #1 **real** (self-contradiction). "No article is used in this emphatic pattern" sat right after the example *el director mismo*. All three statements now say there is no article *directly before mismo*. The when-to-use statement adds "a noun before it keeps its own article".

## es-a2-comparatives-superlatives
- #1 **wrong**. This was already judged wrong in round 2. B&B §6.9 says verbatim that *menor*, unlike *mayor*, is not used for physical size: *esta habitación es más pequeña que esa*, not **menor que esa*. No change.

## es-a2-exclamatives-impersonals
- #1 **real**. "Hace un frío terrible / un calor insoportable" is standard Spanish. B&B has no explicit passage on this, but it is uncontroversial. The callout now says "These nouns take no article after hacer, unless an adjective follows: hace un frío terrible."

## es-a2-indirect-object-pronouns-se
- #1 **real (minor)**. The callout belongs to the IO+DO table, but "always comes first" is false once se is present: B&B §14.2.4 gives the order se > te/os > me/nos > le/lo…, and the page's own example is *se le cayó*. Changed to "always comes before the direct object pronoun".
- #2 **overstated**. B&B §14.10.2 says the redundant pronoun is used in most cases and almost always with proper names, and that omission is rare but possible. Hedged for consistency with the pitfall: "normally requires" became "normally uses", and "— not just «di…»" became "rather than just «di…»".

## es-a2-preterite-yo-spelling
- #1 **real**. *empezé* would be pronounced the same as *empecé*, so for -zar the change is purely a spelling rule (z is not normally written before e). The what-is-it text and the callout now separate the two groups: -car/-gar keep their sound, while -zar follows the spelling rule.
- #2 **real**. The pitfall now reads: "In «llegé» and «buscé» this produces the wrong consonant sound before e; «empezé» sounds the same but breaks the rule that z is not normally written before e."

## es-b1-aspectual-periphrases
- #1 **real**. The gerund rule was given with no exceptions. B&B ch. 16 covers irregular and stem-changing gerunds. The rule now reads "Regular verbs form the gerund…; some change their stem or spelling (leer → leyendo, dormir → durmiendo, pedir → pidiendo)".

## es-b1-discourse-connectors
- #1 **real**. The table pattern put a comma after *o sea que / así que*, which contradicts the page's own example. It is now "Clause A, o sea que / así que Clause B." This matches the example *No tengo dinero, o sea que no puedo ir*.

## es-b1-llevar-time-expressions
- #1 **real**. "I have lived here for three years" is a present perfect. The label is now "present perfect with 'for'".
- #2 **real**. The "active involvement" nuance has no support. B&B §36.3.1 note 1 says the llevar construction is very common in speech but less common in formal writing than *hace … que*. That register contrast replaced the invented nuance.

## es-b1-reciprocal-se
- #1 **real**. B&B §13 (note 2) and §38 give *hablan castellano entre sí* with no se. The callout's rule is now limited to "verbs like ayudar or querer", with an added note: "A verb used without an object, such as hablar, can also take entre sí alone: hablan entre sí, «they talk among themselves»" (gloss from B&B).
- #2 **real**. B&B §38 has *dos personas … se respetan entre sí* (two people), and §30.3 and §10 give *los unos a los otros* for groups. The when-to-use text now reads "el uno al otro / la una a la otra (for two people), los unos a los otros / las unas a las otras (for groups), or entre sí (for two or more)".

## es-b1-relative-clauses
- #1 **real** (self-contradiction with *La casa en la cual vivo*). The pitfall now says "In restrictive clauses with no preposition, use que, not cual". B&B §39.4–39.5 confirms that el cual is fine after prepositions.
- #2 **real (minor)**. B&B §39.3 says que, quien or el cual may all be used in non-restrictive clauses, with quien more likely after a separation or a heavy pause. "Is often preferred over" became "can be used as well as".

## es-b1-ser-estar-uses
- #1 **real**. *Son las tres* is plural. The bullet now says "Use the third person of ser…; in these expressions the verb is singular: es tarde… es mediodía. Clock times agree with the hour: es la una, but son las tres."

## es-b2-conditional-conjecture
- #1 **real**. The future-in-the-past use (*dijo que llegaría*) was missing, and the blanket reading rule would misclassify it. The pitfall now lists three uses, including "the future in the past («dijo que llegaría», «he said he would arrive»)". The reading rule now excludes contexts with a "reporting verb such as dijo que".

## es-b2-relative-clauses-advanced
- #1 **real**. The blanket agreement statement contradicted the table. It now reads "Que, donde and lo que never change form; el/la/los/las que agree with the antecedent in gender and number, and quien/quienes in number only."
