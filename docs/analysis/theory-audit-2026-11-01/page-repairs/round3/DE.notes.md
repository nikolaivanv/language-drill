# DE round-3 claim verdicts

Durrell = Hammer's German Grammar and Usage (local md mirror). Totals: 19 claims, 16 real, 3 overstated, 0 wrong. 14 pages changed (mittelfeld-word-order is not changed).

## de-a1-articles-nominative
- #1 **real** (minor). einen is also used after accusative prepositions. The pitfall now says einen "is for direct objects and after accusative prepositions (für einen Freund) — never for the complement of sein, werden or bleiben". Time phrases were not added.

## de-a1-possessive-articles
- #1 **real**. Durrell §5.2(b)(i)–(ii): the -er of euer is part of the root, and the root -e- drops only when an ending is added. Bare euer is not "uncontracted". The pitfall now says the plural ending is missing, and that once the ending is added the inner -e- drops (eure Kinder).

## de-a1-praeteritum-sein-haben
- #1 **real**. "there is no extra ending" was false for hatte (-e). The formation sentence now says ich war = er war, ich hatte = er hatte, and that er/sie/es has no ending of its own. "fixed paradigms" became "irregular past stems (war-, hatt-) that must be learned".
- #2 **real**. war- is a single stem that takes strong-verb past endings (Durrell ch. 10). The callout no longer says "fully suppletive". It now says the irregular part is the stem (sein → war-), and the endings are the strong ones: none for ich and er/sie/es, then -st, -en, -t, -en.

## de-a2-past-participle-formation
- #1 **real** (minor). The table wording follows Durrell §10.1.2a ("-m or -n after a consonant"; l/r excepted in (iii)), but read literally it gives *gewohnet and *getrennet. The cell now adds atmen → geatmet and "a lengthening h or a double mm/nn does not count: gewohnt, getrennt".

## de-a2-perfekt-with-haben
- #1 **real**. Durrell §10.1.2a gives gearbeitet. The rule now adds "(-et when the stem ends in -t or -d)", which matches the arbeiten example.

## de-a2-verb-preposition-complements
- #1 **real** (obvious typo). The "starts with" cell for auf changed from consonant to vowel.

## de-b1-es-expressions
- #1 **real** (minor). Round 2 already added the casual drop (Tut mir leid) and the rule that es stays when another element comes first (Durrell §3.1b, §16.2.4). Two leftover absolutes are removed: "must always include es" became "include es", and "always paired with es" became "paired with es". The ✗ "Mir tut leid." stays, as in round 2.

## de-b1-futur-i
- #1 **real**. "marks you as a non-native speaker" is false and contradicted the page's own example note. It now says "Futur I is grammatical here, but in these contexts it sounds marked or overly emphatic".

## de-b1-n-declension
- #1 **real**. Durrell §1.3.2: the -e group is nouns "denoting male human beings or animals", and "der Käse and der Charme are regular". The foreign suffixes apply to "most of these (but by no means all)". Edits:
  - The intro now says almost all weak masculines are in these groups, but not every noun in them is weak, so check the dictionary.
  - The -e bullet now says "that denote people or animals" and adds "(der Käse is regular: des Käses)".
  - The foreign bullet now says "Many … mostly ones denoting people" and adds "der Moment and der Kontinent are regular".
  - The Name subtype (things in -e) keeps its own bullet.

## de-b1-passive-werden
- #1 **real**. In the Perfekt passive the finite verb is sein, and in the modal passive it is the modal. The formation sentence now says the forms are built from werden (as the finite verb, or as worden / an infinitive when sein or a modal is finite) plus the participle. The what-is-it sentence ("a form of werden plus the past participle") is accurate, because worden and the infinitive are forms of werden, so it is unchanged.

## de-b2-genitive-prepositions
- #1 **real**. Weak masculines take -(e)n, not -s (Durrell §1.3.2). "Most masculine and neuter singular nouns" now adds "weak masculines take -(e)n instead: aufgrund des Studenten". The pitfall heading is unchanged; it is about forgetting the -s.
- #2 **real**. "Attributive adjectives take -en" holds only after an article. It now reads "after an article take -en … with no article at all, the adjective shows the case itself (infolge starker Regenfälle)". This is consistent with the callout about article-less phrases.

## de-b2-mittelfeld-word-order
- #1 **overstated**. This is the same objection as round-2 #2: the table is introduced as a tendency, and the slot-10 row states the manner-adverbial exception. No change, consistent with the prior verdict.
- #2 **overstated**. This is the same objection as round-2 #3: the slot-5 row already carries the "by default after most adverbials" caveat. The validator's preferred order is one option, not a clear correction. No change, consistent with the prior verdict.

## de-b2-modal-particles-advanced
- #1 **real** (partly). Durrell §9.1.25: warning nur is "more common … when nur is stressed" (bloß patterns with nur, §9.1.5). §9.1.34(b): stressed wohl is concessive. Durrell does not support the claim that reassuring schon is normally stressed, so that part was not used. Edits:
  - What-is-it: "usually unstressed".
  - Formation: "normally unstressed", noting that stressed wohl becomes concessive and that bloß/nur in a warning may be stressed.
  - The eben example gloss changed from "precisely" (Durrell §9.1.8c: the focus use "exactly/precisely", not the particle) to "That's just the problem". This matches the note's self-evidence reading and Durrell's "just" for particle eben.

## de-b2-nominalization
- #1 **overstated**. This is the same claim as round-2 #2: the contraction is required in these fixed patterns (beim Lesen, zum/zur + nominalization). No change.
- #2 **real**. After ein the declension is mixed (einem Kranken). The what-is-it text now reads "weak after der/das, mixed after ein, strong with no article". The formation text now says the ending is strong with no article (etwas Gutes), and after ein it is strong only where ein itself has no ending (ein Kranker, but einem Kranken, einen Kranken).

## de-b2-verb-prefixes
- #1 **real**. Durrell §21 (stress): "The prefix miss- is variable with verbs" (§20.7.3, generally inseparable). miss- is removed from the "always unstressed" list, with the note "miss- is also inseparable, but its stress varies from verb to verb". Not added: Durrell's rare alternative ge-forms (missgeachtet).
