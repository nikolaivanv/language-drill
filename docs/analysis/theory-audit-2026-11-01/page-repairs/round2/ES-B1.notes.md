## es-b1-aspectual-periphrases
- #1 **real**. The rule that the pronoun "must precede the conjugated verb" is false for imperatives (ponte a estudiar) and for infinitives (voy a ponerme a…), where the pronoun attaches to the verb. Rewrote the formation sentence to give all three placements.

## es-b1-collective-agreement
- #1 **real**. B&B §2.3.1(a): the verb is singular "when it immediately follows the noun". §2.3.1(1): plural agreement is "much more common" when the verb is separated from the noun. Replaced the "does not exist / always singular" absolute: a verb directly after a bare collective is singular, and plural only appears at a distance, which learners should avoid. Also changed "or" to "and" in the list.
- #2 **real**. English "everyone knows" is singular, so it cannot cause the error. The cause is now given as the plural meaning («all people»).
- #3 **real**. The warn callout contradicted the page's own "la mayoría de los vecinos creen" (B&B §2.3.1(b)). Restricted the check to bare subject nouns and named the collective + de + plural exception.

## es-b1-deber-obligation-probability
- #1 **overstated**, hedged. Intervening adverbs or subjects (debe usted firmar) are marginal and formal. Changes: the callout now says the infinitive includes haber + participle, "always" became "The basic structure is", and the next sentence now starts "Normally nothing comes between…".

## es-b1-discourse-connectors
- #1 **overstated**, hedged ("usually when the subject of both verbs is the same"). B&B §22.3.2 gives the same-subject rule as learner guidance (e.g. "Enfermó por no comer"), but different-subject uses exist.

## es-b1-indirect-questions
- #1 **real**. B&B §42.3.1(2): Spanish keeps the verb next to the question word in indirect questions ("no sé cuál es la respuesta…"). Unlike English, it does not move the verb to the end. The page's contrast "declarative, not inverted / the verb does not move" was false, and its own examples contradicted it. Rewrote the "what is it" sentence, the formation paragraph and the cuándo example note: the order is the same as in the direct question (verb right after the question word), and copying the English order sounds unnatural.
