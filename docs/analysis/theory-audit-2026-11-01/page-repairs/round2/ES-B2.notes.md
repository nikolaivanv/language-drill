## es-b2-gerund-participle-constructions
- #1 **real**. The ✗ "Nada más llegar yo, tú te fuiste" is a grammatical sentence. B&B §22.3.2(2) gives preposition + infinitive + subject as the best order when the infinitive is used with a different subject. B&B §22.3.2 still recommends the finite clause for learners, and the curriculum describes nada más as same-subject, so same-subject is kept as the default. Changes:
  - "what is it" now says "normally with both verbs sharing…".
  - "when to use it" says a finite clause is "the safe choice", and that if the infinitive is kept the other subject must be stated after it.
  - The pitfall is reframed as "Leaving a different subject unstated": ✗ "Nada más llegar, tú te fuiste." (meaning: as soon as I arrived) / ✓ "En cuanto llegué, tú te fuiste."
- #2 **overstated**, hedged ("is normally the same as the main-clause subject"). Absolute gerunds (estando ella en casa) and gerunds on a perception-verb object are separate patterns.
- #3 **real**. B&B §20.4.7(4): después de que takes the subjunctive for future events. For past events it logically takes the indicative (common in Latin America), though -ra/-se forms are common in Spain. The text now reads "equivalent to a «después de que» or «una vez que» clause with a finite verb".
