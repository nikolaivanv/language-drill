## de-a2-dativ-prepositions
- #1 **real**. The dative plural adds only -n (Durrell §1.2 "-n is added in the dative plural if possible"). The table cell "den + noun -(e)n" became "den + noun -n", which now matches the prose and pitfall.

## de-a2-indefinite-pronouns-basic
- #1 **real**. "Du darfst hier nicht rauchen" is grammatical, and generic du is common in speech. The asterisk is removed and the pitfall reframed: grammatical, but it can sound as if you mean one specific person; man is the neutral, impersonal choice for general rules.

## de-a2-konjunktiv-ii-polite
- #1 **real**. «Würdest du mir helfen können?» is grammatical. The asterisk and "Do not say" are removed; it is now "grammatical but wordy", with könntest preferred.
- #2 **real**. "würde is used with regular verbs" was misleading. Now: with modals (and haben/sein) the one-word Konjunktiv II form is preferred, and würde + infinitive is the usual choice for most other verbs.
- #3 **real**. konnte/könnte have identical endings. The pitfall now says the umlaut is what marks the Konjunktiv II and the endings are the same.
- #4 **real** (in part). sollte is not irregular; it is identical to the simple past. Now: hätte/könnte/würde = hatte/konnte/wurde + umlaut, sollte has no umlaut and looks like the simple past, and all four take the endings of their own simple past. The validator's kam/käme point is out of scope, because the sentence only covers these four forms, which do share their simple-past endings. That part of the claim is wrong.

## de-a2-lassen
- #1 **real**. In subordinate clauses and with modals/perfect, lassen itself goes to the end (Durrell §19.1, verbal bracket). The rule is now limited to a simple main clause, with a parenthesis showing the modal and weil-clause orders.

## de-a2-past-participle-formation
- #1 **real**. fahren → gefahren and essen → gegessen (on the page itself) keep their vowel. Changes: what-is-it now says "end in -en and often change their stem vowel"; the three strong-verb table rule cells now read "stem (vowel often changed)"; the callout says "whether the stem vowel changes — and to what — must be memorised" and adds "(these last two keep their vowel)"; and the pitfall says "and many also change their stem vowel".

## de-a2-perfekt-with-haben
- #1 **real** (partly). "Any verb that **can** take a direct accusative object" is false: fahren takes sein when intransitive (Durrell §10.3.2c(i)). It now reads "A verb **used** with a direct accusative object". "All transitive verbs take haben" is kept, because it is Durrell's own rule (§10.3.2b(i)). The exceptions there (abgehen, durchgehen, loswerden) are beyond A2.
- #2 **real**. sein and bleiben take sein (Durrell §10.3.2a(iv)). The bullet now says "Most intransitive verbs…" and adds "(sein and bleiben are exceptions: they take sein.)".
- #3 **real**. The page's own yes/no example puts haben first. "The structure is always" became "The basic structure is", and the callout now says "second position in a statement (first position in a yes/no question)".
- #4 **real**. The formation and callout statements about the participle being last are now limited to the main clause, with "(in a subordinate clause, haben follows it: …, weil ich gearbeitet habe)".

## de-a2-perfekt-with-sein
- #1 **overstated**. The callout is about the listed motion verbs, not schlafen or arbeiten. Durrell §10.3.2c(ii) allows haben for activity-focused tanzen, segeln and schwimmen, but notes that these are increasingly used with sein. Hedge applied: "the intransitive uses for going somewhere, which take sein" (the "always" is gone).

## de-a2-praeteritum-modals
- #1 **real**. müssen expresses necessity of any kind, and the page's own example is external circumstances. The labels are now "musste (necessity — something had to happen)" and "sollte (an instruction or expectation from someone else)".

## de-a2-reflexive-verbs
- #1 **real**. Yes/no questions are verb-first. Now: "After an adverb at position 1 (or a question word), the verb still comes second; in a yes/no question it comes first." The examples are unchanged.

## de-a2-verb-preposition-complements
- #1 **real**. No preposition governs the nominative. "(wen, wem, wer)" became "(wen, wem)".
