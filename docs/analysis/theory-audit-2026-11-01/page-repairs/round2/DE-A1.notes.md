# DE-A1 round-2 claim verification

Durrell = Hammer's German Grammar and Usage (local md mirror).

## de-a1-negation
- #1 **real**. "nicht stands at the end of the clause, after all other elements" is false whenever there is a second verb part or a complement (Anita hat den Roman nicht gelesen, Durrell §19.6; §19.7 verb complements). Bullet now: nicht goes as late as possible, after objects and time adverbs, but stays before a predicate adjective, a prepositional complement or a second verb part (participle, infinitive, separable prefix), with «Ich habe das Buch nicht gelesen». The callout's "put nicht last" was softened the same way.

## de-a1-plural-formation
- #1 **real** (minor). "always die" is true only for the nominative and accusative (Durrell §1.2, dative plural -n). Now scoped to nom/acc, with "(in the dative it is den: mit den Kindern)".
- #2 **real**. keine is also the feminine singular form (keine Zeit), so it does not "require a plural noun". viele/alle keep "require"; keine now "goes with a plural noun too (keine Bücher), but keine is also the feminine singular form: keine Zeit".

## de-a1-praeteritum-sein-haben
- #1 **real**. The ending list (–, -est, –, …) contradicted the table (ich hatte, er hatte). Now: -e, -est, -e, -en, -et, -en, with ich hatte, du hattest, er hatte.

## de-a1-present-irregular
- #1 **real** (nitpick but literally false: wir sind = sie sind). Changed to "each form has to be learned on its own".
- #2 **overstated**. The three patterns are the core A1 ones and match the curriculum description. One-word hedge added: "The three **main** patterns". laufen/nehmen were not added.

## de-a1-temporal-prepositions
- #1 **real**. Durrell §4.1.1c(i) (contracted forms of the definite article): the uncontracted forms are used when the article is stressed or demonstrative. The formation text now says the contractions are the normal form in ordinary time expressions, and that an dem / in dem appear when the article is stressed ("an dem Tag, an dem wir uns kennengelernt haben"). The example note "obligatory" is now "normal". In the pitfall, "obligatory" became "normal form", and "Always write" became "Write". The ✗ forms *an Montag / *in Juli are still ungrammatical because they have no article at all, so they stay.

## de-a1-v2-word-order
- #1 **real**. Yes/no questions and imperatives are verb-first (Durrell §19.1). The subtitle now says "German statement". The what-is-it section now says "declarative main clause (a statement) and in a W-question" and adds a note that yes/no questions and commands put the verb first.

## de-a1-zero-article
- #1 **real**. An adjective does not bring back ein/eine with plurals or mass nouns (frische Äpfel, frische Milch). The formation sentence is limited to singular profession/nationality nouns after sein/werden. A new paragraph after it says that indefinite plurals and mass nouns stay article-less even with an adjective (Wir kaufen frische Äpfel / Wir brauchen frische Milch). The adjective-present example matches the curriculum ("the article returns with an adjective (Sie ist eine gute Ärztin)").
- #2 **real**. The callout "As soon as an adjective modifies the noun, the article returns" and pitfall 3 are now scoped to singular profession/nationality nouns. Pitfall 3's last sentence now gives the plural/mass contrast ("Sie sind gute Ärztinnen, Wir brauchen frische Milch").
- #3 **overstated**. "In this context" already restricted the claim, and the einige aside explains the meaning difference. A hedge was added anyway: "German has no article corresponding to unstressed English «some»".
