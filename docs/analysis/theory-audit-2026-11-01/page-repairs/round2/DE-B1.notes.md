# DE-B1 round-2 claim verdicts

Totals: 20 claims, 18 real, 1 overstated, 1 wrong. 11 pages changed (de-b1-articles-use is not changed).

## de-b1-adjectives-as-nouns
- #1 real. The -es ending is only nominative/accusative, and "alles Gute" contradicted the -es claim. Rewrote the bullet: -es (nom/acc) after etwas/nichts/viel, dative -em (mit etwas Neuem), and alles + weak -e as a separate sentence. (Durrell §6.2, adjectives used as nouns)
- #2 real. Changed the example note to "-es in the nominative and accusative (dative: mit etwas Neuem)".

## de-b1-articles-use
- #1 overstated. Durrell §4.4.3: "A definite article is always used when names have an adjective with them." Vocatives and letter openings (Liebe Anna) are a different construction, and "ganz Deutschland" uses uninflected ganz, so it is not a counterexample. No change.

## de-b1-dass-clause-perfekt
- #1 real. The bullet now limits the Perfekt to a present-tense main clause and adds that after a past main clause an earlier action normally takes the Plusquamperfekt (the validator's example). (Durrell ch. 12, Plusquamperfekt for anteriority in the past)

## de-b1-dative-reflexive-body
- #1 real. The table has no column of ordinary dative pronouns. The prose now says "The table below shows the dative reflexive forms." The following paragraph already covers ihr/ihm/dem Kind.

## de-b1-es-expressions
- #1 real. Durrell §3.6.2d(ii) says agreement with the real subject applies only to the dummy/placeholder es. The callout now says: with placeholder es the clause is the subject (verb singular); in impersonal and fixed expressions es itself is the subject (always 3sg).
- #2 real. The callout used to say "geben is fixed in this idiom". It now says that what follows es gibt is an accusative object, so a plural noun does not change the verb (es gibt viele Parks).
- #3 real (partly). Durrell §3.1(b): subject es is often dropped in rapid casual speech. Durrell §16.2.4 says es can be omitted only in the sensation and impersonal-passive constructions, so in standard German "Mir tut leid" stays wrong. Edits:
  - The example note now says es is often dropped at the very start in casual speech (Tut mir leid) but stays when another element comes first (Mir tut es leid).
  - The pitfall prose now says es is the subject and stays when another element comes first.
  - "Mir tut es leid" is added to the Correct line, and the ✗ "Mir tut leid." is kept.
  - "cannot be restructured without it" became "paired with es as their subject".

## de-b1-genitive
- #1 real. Durrell §1.3.5a: -s is usual with words ending in a vowel and with foreign words (des Schuhs, des Klubs). The callout now says -es is "a safe choice for native monosyllables ending in a consonant" and adds the -s exceptions.

## de-b1-modal-particles-basic
- #1 real. Durrell ch. 9 (doch; ja §9.1.19): stressed doch is a clear contradiction, and stressed ja is a warning or threat. Edits:
  - The definition now says "usually unstressed".
  - The formation sentence now says "normally unstressed" and adds one line on stressed DOCH and JA.
- #2 real (minor). In "Das ist ja interessant" the subject is in the Vorfeld. The ja and eigentlich rows now read "after finite verb (+ subject)", matching the doch and mal rows.

## de-b1-n-declension
- #1 real. Durrell §1.3.2 lists Nachbar among weak masculines, and its forms are den/dem/des Nachbarn and die Nachbarn. The rule would have produced *Nachbaren. Added "Nachbar also adds only -n, throughout: den Nachbarn, des Nachbarn, die Nachbarn" to the formation section and "(den Nachbarn)" to the when-to-use bullet.
- #2 real (partly). The Name-type genitive (des Namens) is a genuine exception to "must end in -(e)n". The validator's dem Tisch and die Frau points are irrelevant because the check is about weak masculines, and the plural objection is a non-issue. The check now reads "outside the nominative singular, a weak masculine ends in -(e)n (the Name type adds -s in the genitive: des Namens)".
- #3 real. The dictionary tip now reads "weak masculines show -en or -n there (the Name type: -ns), whereas regular masculines show only -s". (Durrell §1.3.3)

## de-b1-schon-noch-erst
- #1 real (reframe). A correct sentence was marked with ❌. It now reads: "«Bist du fertig?» is a correct question, but it misses the point if you mean to express surprise…". The following explanation already said it was neutral.

## de-b1-statt-ohne-zu
- #1 real. The glosses were inverted. They now read "(he did not ask …)" and "(she did not notice …)".
- #2 real, for a different reason than the validator gave. "*ohne sie zu merken" is not ungrammatical: it would mean that he didn't notice her. The page's explanation "he noticed nothing" was imprecise, and the asterisk was misleading. Replaced it with "ohne es zu merken … its understood subject is er, so it would say that he did not notice". This is a correct sentence with a different meaning, and there is no asterisk.
- #3 wrong. The validator's counterexamples (ohne gehört zu werden, Man darf nicht gehen, ohne zu zahlen) all have the same subject in both clauses (er/er, man/man). They do not show ohne…zu with different subjects. No change.
