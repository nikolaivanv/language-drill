# ES-A1 round 2 — claim verdicts

## es-a1-gender-agreement
- #1 **real**. The intro said invariable gentilicios "never change their ending", but the page's own table has marroquí → marroquíes. Changed it to "invariable for gender: same ending in the feminine, only -es in the plural". (B&B §5.2.2(b): adjectives in -í are type 2, with no gender distinction.)
- #2 **real**. "adjectives in -or ... add -a" left out the invariable comparatives. Changed to "-ón, -án, -ín and most in -or", and added: mayor, menor, mejor, peor (like superior, interior) are invariable for gender, e.g. *una hermana mayor*. (B&B §5.2.1 note 2: the eleven comparative -or adjectives are type 2.)

## es-a1-hay-estar
- #1 **real**. "The noun must be accompanied by a definite article, a possessive, or a demonstrative" is contradicted by the page's own table (Yo estoy en casa) and leaves out names. Reworded to "a specific, definite thing or person: a noun with def. art./possessive/demonstrative, or a name or a pronoun (María está en casa, yo estoy aquí)".
- #2 **real** (it contradicts the corrected curriculum). The intro said the choice "is not optional; the wrong one is ungrammatical". The curriculum description now says "locatable people and things can take estar even when indefinite", and B&B §34.3.2 (estar for mobile things: *Han estado aquí tres presidentes*) agrees. Changes:
  - Replaced the absolute with "not a matter of style: to introduce something new, Spanish uses hay", plus a parenthetical: abstract nouns take only hay; a person or movable thing can take estar while indefinite (*Un señor está en la puerta*).
  - In the pitfall, kept `*está una farmacia cerca` (a fixed place, so still wrong) and narrowed "Reserve estar" to places and things, adding the person/movable exception.
  - Left `*hay el banco` unchanged; the claim did not dispute it.

## es-a1-negation-tampoco
- #1 **real**. «No, tengo» glossed as «No, I do have one» is not a reliable affirmative; it is a self-contradictory reply. Changes:
  - The pitfall is now framed as an incomplete negative reply. The heading reads "in a negative reply", and the prose says that without the second no the verb is affirmative, so the reply contradicts itself.
  - The callout question is now plain «¿Tienes coche?», which removes the negative-question complication.
  - The gloss now reads «"No" says no, but "tengo" says "I have"». The ✗ form is still genuinely wrong.

## es-a1-porque-para
- #1 **real (narrow)**. The intro "para + infinitive ... always followed by the bare infinitive" describes the construction itself, so I left it. The callout "**para** = one word, always followed by an infinitive" is false (para ti, para mañana). Changed it to "when it states a purpose, it is followed by an infinitive". The curriculum scopes this point to para + infinitive of purpose, which is consistent.

## es-a1-possessives-atonic
- #1 **real**. The asterisk on *me duele mi cabeza* conflicted with the pitfall's "grammatically possible". B&B §9.3.4 note 4 gives *me duele mi cabeza* as colloquial Mexican, and project convention treats regional usage as correct, so the asterisk was the wrong framing. Removed the asterisk in both spots. The "when to use" bullet now says "rather than *me duele mi cabeza*, which sounds unnatural in standard Spanish". The pitfall now says "heard colloquially (especially in Mexico) but unnatural in standard Spanish".
- #2 **real**. "always comes immediately before the noun" is false with *mi nueva casa*. Changed it to "always comes before the noun (an adjective can stand in between: mi nueva casa)". Also dropped "directly" from the intro ("placed before a noun").

## es-a1-present-indicative-regular
- #1 **real**. "word order alone signals the question" is false; ¿Hablas español? has statement word order. Now reads: "the word order is the same as in the statement, and only intonation (and, in writing, the question marks) signals the question."
