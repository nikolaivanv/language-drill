# ES-A2 round 2 — claim verdicts

## es-a2-comparatives-superlatives
- #1 **real** (under the project's regional-usage convention). The pitfall marked `*más grande` as wrong for age. *Más grande* = "older" is common Latin American usage. B&B §6.8(b) gives only *mayor* for 'older' but does not condemn it. Changed the ✗ code span to plain em: "mayor for age (más grande is also heard for age, especially in Latin America, but mayor is the standard choice)". *Unsure-ish*: B&B has no explicit statement either way, so this rests on the regional-usage convention.
- #2 **wrong**. B&B §6.9 says verbatim: "*Menor*, unlike *mayor*, is not used for physical size: *esta habitación es más pequeña que esa*, not *\*menor que esa*". It also says mayor for physical size "is more characteristic of written language" (§6.8a). The callout matches the book. *de menor tamaño / en menor medida* are the dimension/quantity uses B&B allows separately, and the callout's ✗ (*esta caja es menor que la otra*) is a physical-size comparison. No change.
- #3 **real**. "Use de only before numbers or quantities" is a false absolute; B&B §6.6 requires *más/menos de lo que* before clauses. Dropped "only" and added "and in the pattern más/menos de lo que before a clause (más de lo que pensaba)".

## es-a2-connectors

Changed it to "A period, a semicolon or a comma separates the two clauses …; when the connector opens a new sentence, it simply follows the period". ## es-a2-diacritic-pairs
- #1 **real**. The mí rule "after a preposition" is false for *con mi madre*. It now reads "when the word is the pronoun after a preposition, with no noun following it: a mí, para mí, de mí (but con mi madre, where mi is the possessive before a noun)".
- #2 **real**. "if the word is a pronoun or verb, it needs the accent" is false (te, se, me). It now reads "if the word is the pronoun or verb member of one of these pairs".
- #3 **real**. "always a subject pronoun, verb form, or adverb / always ... possessive, preposition, conjunction, or particle" is contradicted by té (noun), mí (prepositional pronoun), el (article) and te (pronoun). Reworded: the accented member "carries its own stress — usually a stressed pronoun, a verb form, or an adverb (té, a noun, is the odd one out)". The bare member is "usually an unstressed function word: a possessive, an article, a preposition, a conjunction, or an unstressed pronoun".
- #4 **overstated (hedged)**. Interrogatives (qué/que …) are a separate topic, and the claim's "sí que vs. pero si" is not a pair. Still, "every standard pair" is a false absolute, so I changed it to "the main pairs".
- #5 **real**. The callout glossed *solo* as "alone". The accent was only ever on the adverb *sólo* 'only'; the adjective *solo* 'alone' never takes one. "Written without an accent in all uses" also ignores the RAE's current tolerance of *sólo* where there is a real risk of ambiguity. Rewritten: per the RAE Ortografía (2010), the adverb *solo* («only») and the demonstrative pronouns no longer need the accent and are normally written without it, with an accented *sólo* still tolerated where there is a real risk of ambiguity. The adjective *solo* («alone»: volvió sola) never takes an accent.

## es-a2-present-irregular-stem-changes
- #1 **real**. vencer → venzo was listed under "-ger, -gir, -guir". The bullet now reads "verbs in -ger, -gir, -guir, and in -cer after a consonant, change before -o" (c→z before o/a: venzo, convenzo, ejerzo).

## es-a2-preterite-stem-spelling
- #1 **real**. "i between two vowels → y" is contradicted by leí/leíste/leímos in the page's own table. Added "unstressed" in all three spots: the intro (with "(the -ió and -ieron endings)"), the when-to-use bullet ("the unstressed ending i") and the pitfall ("An unstressed i … (a stressed í stays: leí, leímos)"). This is consistent with the curriculum ("y between vowels … Only 3sg/3pl are affected").

## es-a2-reflexive-verbs
- #1 **real**. The prose ("third-person pronoun with a first-person plural verb") was the reverse of the example, and *Nos levanta* is grammatical ('he/she gets us up'). Changes:
  - The prose now reads "Keeping the third-person verb ending after a first-person plural pronoun is a common slip."
  - The ✗ is now `Nosotros nos levanta.`, which is genuinely wrong because the explicit subject forces the person.
  - The ✓ is now *Nosotros nos levantamos.*
