# DE-B2 round-2 claim verdicts

Totals: 32 claims, 26 real, 4 overstated, 2 wrong. 13 pages changed.

## de-b2-causal-connectors
- #1 real. Only nominalized infinitives are always neuter (der Regen and die Erhöhung are not). The callout now says "Nominalized infinitives are neuter…".
- #2 real. A da-clause can come first. The note now says folglich marks the consequence, whereas weil/da mark the cause, instead of claiming the order is reversed.

## de-b2-conditional-connectors
- #1 real. The page's own es sei denn and sonst rows put the main clause first. The formation sentence now limits "conditional first, then finite verb" to wenn/falls, verb-first, bei and im Falle, and says the main clause comes first with es sei denn and sonst/andernfalls.
- #2 real. After a fronted conditional, plain subject-first order is not standard. The bullet now says the main clause follows "with its finite verb first (inverted order), optionally after a resumptive dann or so".
- #3 real (terminology). "coordinating adverb" became "conjunctional adverb that fills the first position of its clause".

## de-b2-dass-equivalents
- #1 real. "Including the correlate is never wrong" is false for sich freuen (glad vs. looking forward). Durrell §16.5.14 lists sich freuen auf/über among verbs whose correlate is often dropped, and that is where the ambiguity comes from. The pitfall now:
  - lists hoffen auf, sich erinnern an and aufpassen auf (all in Durrell's "often left out" list) as optional;
  - uses "Ich hoffe (darauf), dass…" as the example;
  - warns that the correlate changes the meaning with sich freuen, so it should not be added by default.
- #2 real. The page's own glosses differ ("glad" vs. "looking forward"). Edits:
  - The when-to-use bullet now uses sich erinnern an (daran) as the emphasis-only example and adds the freuen / freuen darauf meaning contrast.
  - The table note and the example note now say darauf makes "look forward to" explicit.
- #3 real. The prose now says the first three rows use hoffen (hoffen auf for the nominal phrase) and the correlate row uses sich freuen auf.

## de-b2-extended-attributes
- #1 real. Further adjectives can follow the participle (der von uns gekaufte neue Wagen). Edits:
  - Dropped "strict" from the formation sentence and added the adjective note there.
  - The pitfall now says "only further adjectives may stand between the participle and the noun" instead of "immediately before".
- #2 real. "After the verb in a relative clause" was wrong for a verb-final clause. It now says that all other elements of the relative clause go before the participle, the relative pronoun and auxiliary drop, and the active subject becomes a von-phrase (den wir gekauft haben → von uns gekauft).
- #3 real. "Like a predicative adjective in attributive position" became "like an attributive adjective".
- #4 real (one word). "bare participle stem" became "bare participle form".

## de-b2-indefinite-pronouns
- #1 wrong. Durrell §5.5.23: sämtliche "is used, as a determiner or a pronoun, in the plural only". No change.
- #2 real. A grammatical sentence carried an asterisk. Removed the asterisk from "Ja, es ist noch etwas da."; the "is not wrong, but shifts the meaning" framing stays.
- #3 wrong. "*Ich gehe irgendwo" for motion to an unspecified place is indeed nonstandard (irgendwohin). "Wir gehen irgendwo essen" is a locative of essen, a different construction. No change.

## de-b2-konjunktiv-i
- #1 real. habe and werde are regular (stem + -e), and only sei is irregular. The callout now says so, and it adds that werden forms the future in indirect speech.
- #2 overstated, hedged. Durrell §14.4.3c(ii): the indicative is almost as frequent as the subjunctive after dass in writing, but "if dass is omitted … the subjunctive is almost always used". The page's example has no dass, so flagging it is defensible. Softened "non-standard" and "always" to "breaks the norm of reporting style", with the dass/no-dass distinction. The callout's "not optional — it is the rule" became "this replacement is the norm" (Durrell calls it the replacement rule).

## de-b2-konjunktiv-ii
- #1 real. A resumptive dann/so is standard. The bullet and pitfall now allow "or after a resumptive dann/so".

## de-b2-mittelfeld-word-order
- #1 real. The callout contradicted the page's own first example (one pronoun, ihn, placed in slot 2). It now says that any unstressed object pronoun goes in slot 2/3, and that accusative-before-dative ordering matters only with two pronouns.
- #2 overstated. The slot-10 row already says "before manner adverbials (slot 8)", and the intro calls the table a tendency, with a pointer to the slot-10 note. No change.
- #3 overstated. The slot-5 row already carries the "by default after most adverbials" caveat, and the intro points to it. No change.
- #4 real (minor). The note now calls nach Berlin "place/direction … strictly a direction complement of fahren, which likewise stands last". This is consistent with the "nicht nach Berlin" complement treatment.

## de-b2-modal-connectors
- #1 real. The self-check now says "a noun phrase must follow (a noun, article, adjective, possessive or object pronoun: durch ihn, durch seine Arbeit) — never a subject pronoun starting a clause".

## de-b2-nominalization
- #1 real. A dass-clause is not a relative clause, so "a full relative clause" became "a full clause".
- #2 overstated. In these fixed patterns (beim Lesen, zum/zur + nominalization) the contraction is in fact required. The uncontracted forms only appear with a stressed or demonstrative article, which is outside these patterns. No change.

## de-b2-noun-verb-collocations
- #1 real (partly). "The noun phrase does not change" contradicted "eine wichtige Rolle spielen". It now says verb, preposition and article stay fixed but many nouns can take an adjective. I kept the "fixed article" wording because the corrected curriculum description says "the verb and its article/preposition are fixed". Article variation (die/keine Entscheidung) is not added.
- #2 real. The heading "Passive-like constructions" became "Stative and causative pairs". "experiences" became "is in the state or brings it about", in the bullet and in the zur Verfügung stehen example note.
- #3 real. "the default phrasing" became "typical of". "Informal speech might say entscheiden" became "neutral style often uses a simple verb such as entscheiden".

## de-b2-relatives-advanced
- #1 real (nuanced). Durrell §5.4.5(a) says a correlating demonstrative "can be added" when cases differ, and gives an example without it: "Wen es zum Lehrerberuf hinzieht, bevorzugt…". That works because the main clause needs a nominative. When the main clause needs an oblique case (dem kann ich nicht helfen), the resumptive is required. The bullet and formation now say that when the cases differ the main clause begins with der/dem/den, which may be dropped only when the main clause needs a nominative. This stays consistent with the curriculum's "wer …, (der) …".
- #2 real. "case of the subject in the main clause" became "the case the main clause requires".
- #3 real. The page itself teaches wo(r)- compounds for clausal antecedents. The pitfall now says "Only was (or, after a preposition, a wo(r)- compound such as worüber)", and the example note now says "Only was (not das)". Dated welches is not added.

## de-b2-text-reference-words
- #1 real. Durrell §3.5.2(b): außer, gegenüber, ohne and seit form no prepositional adverb, and "ohne es" is possible. Edits:
  - Added a sentence after the -r- rule listing them, with außerdem and seither as the replacements for außer and seit.
  - The pitfall now says "normally does not allow preposition + es" and "For the prepositions that form one, the da(r)-compound is obligatory".
