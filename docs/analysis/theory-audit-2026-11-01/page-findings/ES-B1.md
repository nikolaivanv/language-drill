## es-a2-comparatives-superlatives

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → callout: "For size, you can use either the regular forms (_más grande_, _más pequeño_) or the irregular forms (_mayor_, _menor_)."
- **Problem**: *Menor* is not used for physical size: *esta habitación es más pequeña que esa*, not \**menor que esa*. *Mayor* for physical size is possible but restricted (mostly written, not for small things). A learner following the callout will produce \*"esta caja es menor que la otra".
- **Source**: B&B §6.9 ("*Menor*, unlike *mayor*, is not used for physical size"); §6.8a

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls: "Say mejor, not \*más bueno; peor, not \*más malo"
- **Problem**: *Más bueno / más malo* are correct when talking about moral qualities (kind, naughty): *es el más malo de todos*, *tu papá es más bueno que el mío*. Starring them as always wrong overstates the rule. It holds for quality ("better/worse"), not for goodness of character.
- **Source**: B&B §6.3.1 note 3; §6.2 note 5

## es-b1-deber-obligation-probability

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: what is it?: "the presence or absence of **de** … changes the meaning of the sentence entirely". Also pitfall "Treating deber and deber de as interchangeable … «Deben terminar» (they must finish — obligation)"
- **Problem**: Only one direction is a real error: *deber de* for obligation is non-standard. *Deber* without *de* for supposition (*debe ser tarde*, *debió ser vergüenza*) is now so widespread that the NGLE accepts it, though it prefers *deber de*. The page itself says "the optional **de**" in the formation callout, which contradicts the "changes the meaning entirely" framing. A learner will wrongly mark *Deben ser las cinco* (= probably five o'clock) as obligation-only or wrong.
- **Source**: B&B §25.3.2 note 1; §25.3.1 note 1

## es-b1-discourse-connectors

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls: "Using the subjunctive here — _\*aunque llueva, salimos_ — shifts the meaning to a hypothetical («even if it rains») and is incorrect when the rain is already happening."
- **Problem**: *Aunque* + subjunctive is also used with known or real facts. It makes the concession stronger, or dismisses the fact as irrelevant: *aunque sea tu padre, no tiene derecho*; *perdieron aunque jugaran bien*. So the subjunctive is not "incorrect" when the fact is real. It is a different nuance (discounting the fact), not an error.
- **Source**: B&B §20.4.10 note 1 (and the example *Perdieron aunque jugaran/jugasen bien*)

## es-b1-imperative-negative-pronouns

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it: "Adding one or two pronouns shifts the stress count, so a written accent is required". Also pitfalls: "When one or two pronouns are added to an affirmative imperative, the original stressed syllable of the verb must carry a written accent"
- **Problem**: A monosyllabic imperative plus **one** enclitic takes no accent: *dime, dame, hazlo, ponlo, vete*. The page's own table shows *dime* and *vete* without accents, so it contradicts itself. The accent rule is not "always with one or two pronouns". It follows the normal stress rules: *dámelo, cómpralo, díselo* take an accent, while *dime, dale, ponte* do not.
- **Source**: B&B ch. 44 accent rules; §21 (*dame la mano*)

## es-b1-impersonal-plural

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → callout: "The verb agrees with its grammatical object only when that object is a direct-object noun — the verb itself stays in the third-person plural regardless."
- **Problem**: This sentence is self-contradictory and states a false rule: Spanish verbs never agree with their object. In the impersonal 3rd-plural construction the verb is always 3rd plural, whatever the object (*me robaron la cartera*, *venden pan*). Remove the first clause.
- **Source**: expert knowledge (B&B §32 on impersonal 3rd plural)

## es-b1-indirect-questions

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it: "Same subject in both clauses → infinitive … This construction is not available with **si**." The first example then reads "No sé **si ir** a la fiesta esta noche", with the note "Yes/no indirect question with **si** + infinitive (same subject)".
- **Problem**: The page contradicts itself, and the prose is the wrong half. *Si* + infinitive is fully grammatical (*no sé si ir*, *dudaba si aceptar*). Delete the "not available with si" sentence. The formation table row "yes/no … si | finite clause" is also incomplete.
- **Source**: expert knowledge (NGLE §43.3; standard usage)

## es-b1-influence-verbs-infinitive

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it? / formation: "pairs the influence verb with a _dative clitic_ (me, te, le, nos, os, les)". Formation pattern: "[subject] + [dative clitic] + [influence verb] + [infinitive]"
- **Problem**: When *dejar* or causative *hacer* is followed by an **intransitive** infinitive, the standard clitic is accusative (*lo/la*): *la hice bajar*, *lo/la dejaron ir*, *la hiciste reír*. *Le* is used here in leísta Spain (masc.) or with transitive infinitives (*le hice tomar un café*). Calling the slot "dative" and listing *le/les* teaches \**le hizo llorar* for a woman, which is the feminine-leísmo pattern the project treats as a defect. The page's examples all use *me/nos*, so they hide the problem.
- **Source**: B&B §15.6.5 note 2; §20.3.8 note 3

- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation: "When the alternative **que + subjunctive** construction is used, the dative clitic is dropped … «le permitieron entrar» → «permitieron que entrara»."

- **Confidence**: medium

## es-b1-llevar-time-expressions

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: pitfalls: "Say Hace un año que trabajo aquí, not _\*Hace un año que trabajé aquí_ or _\*Hace un año que trabajaba aquí_."
- **Problem**: Both starred sentences are grammatical. *Hace un año que trabajé aquí* = "it's a year since I worked here" (hace … que + preterite = 'ago/since'). *Hace … que* + imperfect is also used, with a change of meaning (B&B note 3). They are wrong only for the intended "have been working" meaning. Presenting them as ungrammatical will mislead learners about the 'ago' construction.
- **Source**: B&B §36.4 (hace + preterite = 'ago'); §36.3.2 note 3

## es-b1-nominalizers

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation → callout: "The contraction **del** only occurs when _de_ is followed directly by the article _el_ before a noun … In the nominalizer, _el_ is the pronominalized article, so no contraction applies."
- **Problem**: *De* + nominalizer *el* does contract: *la del que la contempla*, *el libro del que te hablé*, *mi coche y el del vecino*. The claim that "del" happens only before a noun, and never with the nominalizer *el*, is false and would produce \**la de el que…*.
- **Source**: B&B §40.1.4 note 1 (*la del que la contempla, no la del que la programa*)

## es-b1-pluperfect

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it: "Words and phrases such as _ya_, _todavía no_, _cuando_, _antes de que_ (before), and _después de que_ (after) frequently signal that the pluperfect is needed."
- **Problem**: *Antes de que* always takes the subjunctive, so it can never introduce the indicative *había* + participle that this page teaches. *Después de que* in the past usually takes the preterite or the -ra subjunctive. Listing them as pluperfect triggers invites \**antes de que había llegado* (correct: *antes de que llegara / hubiera llegado*).
- **Source**: B&B §20.4.7 (and note 4)

## es-b1-present-subjunctive

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation: "For e→ie and o→ue verbs, the change occurs in all forms except **nosotros** and **vosotros**. For -ir verbs with e→i or o→u changes, the stem change applies to all forms."
- **Problem**: (1) There is no "o→u" class with u in all forms. *Dormir/morir* have *ue* in the stressed forms and *u* in nosotros/vosotros: *duerma, duermas, durmamos, durmáis, duerman*. Following the page gives \**durma*. (2) -ir e→ie verbs (*sentir, preferir, mentir*) do change in nosotros/vosotros: *sintamos, prefiramos*. The page's rule gives \**sentamos/\*sentemos*-type errors.
- **Source**: B&B §16.6.8, §16.11.18 (dormir/morir); ch. 16 radical-changing -ir verbs

## es-b1-reported-speech

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it?: "When the reporting verb introduces a command, the reported clause uses **que + present subjunctive**, regardless of whether the reporting verb is in the present or the past." Formation row: "«¡Ven!» → dice/dijo que vengas". Pitfall: "always **que + present subjunctive**".
- **Problem**: With a past reporting verb the normal sequence is the **imperfect** subjunctive: *dijo que vinieras/vinieses*, *le dije que se fuera*. *Dijo que vengas* is possible only when the order still applies now, and it is not the default. This also contradicts the page's own present→imperfect tense shift for statements.
- **Source**: B&B §20.3.2 (tense-agreement table: *Le dije que se fuera/fuese*); §20.3.7 note 3 (*dijo que se terminara*)

## es-b1-ser-estar-uses

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: what is it? / when to use it: "impersonal **ser** for time of day and atmospheric conditions (_es tarde, es de noche_)"
- **Problem**: "Atmospheric conditions" in Spanish are expressed with *hacer* or *estar* (*hace frío, está nublado, hace sol*), not *ser*. Only time and day/night expressions (*es de noche, es de día*) take *ser*. The label invites \**es frío*, \**es nublado*.
- **Source**: expert knowledge (B&B ch. 33)
