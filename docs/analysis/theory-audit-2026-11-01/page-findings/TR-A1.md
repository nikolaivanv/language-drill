## tr-a1-beri-dir
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls → "Mixing up -DEn beri and -DIr for spans": "the ablative of saat is saattan, giving iki saattan beri — which is valid"; and "Using için for elapsed duration": "Use iki saattir bekliyorum or iki saattan beri bekliyorum instead."
- **Problem**: *saat* is a loanword with front-vowel suffix harmony (saatte, saati, saatler, **saatten**). The correct form is *iki saatten beri*. "saattan" is wrong, and the page contradicts itself: it correctly writes *saattir* (front i) in the same section.
- **Source**: G&K §3.4(iv) (loanwords that take front-vowel suffixes despite a back final vowel, e.g. hakikat-siz)

## tr-a1-accusative-definite-object
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → "The suffix follows two-way vowel harmony based on the last vowel of the stem"
- **Problem**: -(y)I is an I-type suffix with **four-way** harmony (ı/i/u/ü). The page's own intro and table show four variants, so the sentence contradicts them.
- **Source**: G&K §3.2.1

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it → "Pronouns. Personal pronouns (and other pronouns) are inherently definite and always take the accusative"
- **Problem**: The words "and other pronouns" are too broad. Indefinite and negative pronouns are not inherently definite and are normally unmarked as objects: *Bir şey aldım*, *Hiçbir şey görmedim*. The claim holds for personal and demonstrative pronouns only.
- **Source**: expert knowledge (G&K ch. 22 on specificity)

## tr-a1-ablative-dative
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls callout → "if the last consonant of the stem is voiceless, the ablative starts with t; otherwise it starts with d."
- **Problem**: The rule depends on the stem's final **sound**, not its last consonant. For a vowel-final stem like *kapı* or *masa*, the "last consonant" (p, s) is voiceless, but the form is *kapıdan* or *masadan*, not *kapıtan*. The page's own formation section says "stem ends in a voiceless consonant", which is correct.
- **Source**: G&K §8.1 / expert knowledge

## tr-a1-dan-a-kadar
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it → "Both halves are required; neither can stand alone." and common pitfalls → "Dropping kadar. «sabahtan akşama» is incomplete. The postposition kadar is not optional — without it the «to / until» meaning is lost."
- **Problem**: Both claims are false.
  - *-A kadar* stands alone all the time, e.g. *Cumaya kadar biter*, *Saat bire kadar beklerim*.
  - *-DAn … -A* without kadar is fully grammatical and idiomatic, e.g. *sabahtan akşama çalışıyor*, *İstanbul'dan Ankara'ya gittim*. The dative alone already gives the "to" meaning.
- **Source**: G&K §17.2.2(iii) (examples 29–32: kadar with a dative complement, no ablative partner)

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: examples → target "Pazartesi'den cuma'ya kadar okul var." with note "Day names use an apostrophe before the suffix when written with a capital letter."
- **Problem**: Day names are common nouns and take suffixes without an apostrophe: *pazartesiden cumaya kadar*. G&K writes *Pazartesiden beri*, *Cumaya kadar*. The lowercase "cuma'ya" is also inconsistent with the stated rule, and the page's own formation table writes *pazartesiden* and *cumaya* with no apostrophe.

## tr-a1-clock-time-dates
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it → "Days of the week take no case suffix at all."; common pitfalls → "Days of the week never take a case suffix."
- **Problem**: This is only true of the bare time-adverbial use ("on Wednesday"). Day names take case suffixes freely in other functions: *pazartesiden beri*, *cumaya kadar*, *salıyı bekliyorum*. The sister page tr-a1-dan-a-kadar even teaches *pazartesiden … cumaya kadar*. Restrict the rule to "on <day>".
- **Source**: G&K §17.2.3 ex. (38), §17.2.2 ex. (30)

## tr-a1-genitive-possessive
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it → "Neither suffix can be dropped without producing ungrammatical Turkish."; pitfalls → "Writing *öğretmen kitabı instead of öğretmenin kitabı produces an indefinite compound…"
- **Problem**: There are two problems.
  - The pitfall itself says that dropping the genitive gives a valid indefinite compound, so *öğretmen kitabı* ("teacher's book" as a type) is grammatical. It should not carry an asterisk or be called ungrammatical.
  - A pronominal genitive possessor is routinely dropped: *kitabı* "his/her book", *Kedisi var*.
  
  Only the possessive suffix on the head is obligatory.
- **Source**: G&K ch. 10 (noun compounds, -(s)I without genitive); expert knowledge (pro-drop of the genitive pronoun)

## tr-a1-locative
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: examples → "I am at home" note: "ev (home) ends in the voiced consonant v → -de; back vowel → -de."
- **Problem**: *e* is a **front** vowel. That is why the suffix is -de, as the page's own formation rule says. The note gives the wrong reason for the right form.

## tr-a1-var-yok
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → "Because var ends in a vowel, the suffix is -dı → vardı."
- **Problem**: *var* ends in the voiced consonant *r*. That is why the suffix keeps d. The page's stated "vowel" reason would mislead learners about the d/t rule.
- **Source**: G&K §1.1 / §8 (consonant assimilation)

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: examples → "Vaktim yok." note: "«Vakit» … takes the 1st-person suffix «-im» (vowel harmony: back rounded → «-im» after front unrounded vowel)."
- **Problem**: The harmony explanation is self-contradictory. The suffix is -im because the last vowel of *vakit* is *i* (front unrounded); nothing here is "back rounded". The note also omits the actual stem change, vowel drop *vakit → vakt-*, which is the non-obvious part of *vaktim*.
- **Source**: G&K §3.2.1; §2.3 (vowel/Ø alternation)

## tr-a1-vowel-harmony
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls → "Treating loanwords as front-vowel words. Some borrowed words contain front vowels but take back-vowel suffixes … For example, oteller (hotels) uses -ler, but lokaller may be lokallar depending on usage."
- **Problem**: The loanword exception runs the other way: some loanwords with a **back** final vowel take **front** suffixes, e.g. *saat → saatler*, *hal → halim*, *gol → golü*, *etol → etolden*. *otel → oteller* is ordinary harmony. *lokal* takes front suffixes (*lokaller*, palatal l), and "lokallar" is not standard. As written, the pitfall teaches a non-existent rule and a wrong form.
- **Source**: G&K §3.4(iii)–(iv)

## tr-a1-instrumental-ile
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → "Personal pronouns do not take -(y)lA directly. They first take the genitive suffix…"; pitfalls → "*benle, *senle, and *onla are all incorrect."
- **Problem**: There are two problems.
  - The plural pronoun *onlar* takes -lA without a genitive: *onlarla*, not *onlarınla*. The plural forms of the genitive-attracting pronouns (*bizler*, *sizler*, *onlar*) stay non-case-marked. The page's table silently omits *onlar*.
  - *benle*, *senle* and *onla* are not ungrammatical. They are the normal colloquial forms, and G&K lists them as everyday speech. They should be described as informal, not starred as incorrect.
- **Source**: G&K §8.1.4 ("In everyday speech, -(y)lA may be attached directly to pronouns… benle"); §17.2.1 (plural-marked pronouns excluded); §6 ("↓onla 'with him/her'")

## tr-a1-gore-bence
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation → "Note that a -CE form exists only for 1st and 2nd person"; pitfalls → "There is no once … meaning «in his/her/their opinion»."
- **Problem**: G&K explicitly lists *onca* "according to him/her". It is rarer and formal, but it exists. *-CA* also attaches to nouns with the same viewpoint sense, e.g. *bakanlıkça*. The page's statement that *onlarca* means "dozens" and not "in their opinion" is correct. The categorical "no 3rd-person form" is not.
- **Source**: G&K §6 (list: "onca 'according to him/her'"); §16.1.6(vi)

## tr-a1-future
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it → "For predictions and decisions, -(y)AcAk is the only choice."
- **Problem**: Spontaneous decisions and offers made at the moment of speaking are very commonly expressed with the aorist: *Tamam, ben yaparım*, *Çay içerim*. Calling -(y)AcAk "the only choice" for decisions would lead learners to mark the natural aorist offer as wrong.
- **Source**: G&K §21.3 (aorist: willingness / offers); expert knowledge

## tr-a1-questions
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it → "The person ending attaches to mI, not to the verb."; callout → "In all other tenses and in nominal predicates, mI takes the person ending."
- **Problem**: The rule as stated has a 3rd-person-plural exception that the page's own table shows: -lAr stays on the predicate, as in *geliyorlar mı?* and *öğrenciler mi?*. The rule of thumb should exempt 3pl -lAr, which always precedes mI.
- **Source**: G&K §19.1 / §8.4 (position of -lAr vs. mI)

## tr-a1-personal-pronouns
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it → "Location (locative) — use the locative form to indicate that something is at or on the pronoun's referent (rare with personal pronouns, but grammatically possible)."
- **Problem**: The locative of personal pronouns is very frequent. It is the standard way to say "X is with/on me" or "I have X (on me)": *Kitap bende*, *Anahtar sende mi?*. Calling it "rare" hides a core A1 use.
- **Source**: expert knowledge

## tr-a1-stem-changes
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation → vowel-drop table: "akıl | akl- | aklı | his/her mind (acc.)" and "burun | burn- | burnu | its nose (acc.)"
- **Problem**: The glosses conflate two readings. *aklı* is either "his/her mind" (3sg possessive, nominative) or "the mind" (accusative). "His/her mind (acc.)" would be *aklını*, and "its nose (acc.)" would be *burnunu*.
- **Source**: G&K §8.1 (pronominal n before case after 3sg possessive)
