## es-b2-appreciative-suffixes
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation — "-ecito (after words ending in -e or containing a diphthong ie/ue in the final syllable)"; table row "puerta | ue- diphthong | puertecita"
- **Problem**: The diphthong condition applies to the **first** syllable of a two-syllable word (puerta, piedra, nieto, siesta, viejo), not the final syllable. The page's own example *puerta* has *ue* in its first syllable. Also, this is a Peninsular pattern: in Latin America these words usually take plain -ito (*puertita, piedrita, viejito, nietito*). So the pitfall "Applying the extended allomorph to a plain-vowel base is a clear error" combined with the table leaves the learner thinking *puertita* is wrong.
- **Source**: B&B §43.2.1 (1) "words ending in -e or having the diphthong -ie or -ue in their first syllable… take the form in -c-"; same section on Latin-American *puertita, piedrita*.

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation — "-cito (after words ending in -n or -r)"; pitfall "triggered only by specific endings (-n, -r, or final -e / ie-ue diphthong)"
- **Problem**: -cito applies to words of **more than one syllable** ending in -n/-r (camioncito, mujercita). ## es-b2-causal-connectors
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "*Están en casa ya que veo luz encendida* (unnatural — ya que introduces a shared accepted cause not a personal observation used as evidence)"; also when-to-use — "[enunciative porque] cannot be replaced by ya que or puesto que without changing the meaning"
- **Problem**: *Ya que* and *puesto que* are the typical connectors for evidential or justifying ("external") causal clauses. *Debe de haber llovido, ya que el suelo está mojado* and *Están en casa, ya que hay luz* are fully natural. Starring this use teaches the learner that a normal construction is ungrammatical.
- **Source**: B&B §37.5.2 (ya que / puesto que = "since, given that, seeing that"); expert knowledge (RAE NGLE §46.5 causales externas: ya que, puesto que, como, pues).

## es-b2-clitic-advanced
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it — "When a direct-object noun phrase is moved before the verb for emphasis or topic-marking Spanish requires a resumptive clitic… neither can be omitted"
- **Problem**: The redundant clitic is obligatory only with **definite or specific** fronted objects. With indefinite or bare objects and with quantifiers it is not used: *Carne no como*, *Mucha prisa ha debido tener*, *¡Cuántas tonterías dices!*, *Eso creo yo*. Stated as absolute, the rule leads learners to produce *Carne la no como*.
- **Source**: B&B §14.10.1 and notes (1)–(2).

- **Problem**: Mixed counterfactuals with a simple conditional are standard when the consequence is in the present: *Si hubieras trabajado más, no tendrías que ir a clase ahora*. The page forbids them.

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium

## es-b2-conditional-connectors
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: what is it — "each one governs a fixed mood"; table "siempre que | subjunctive | provided that / as long as"
- **Problem**: *Siempre que* + indicative is extremely common and means "whenever" (*la saludaba siempre que la veía*). Because the page says the mood is fixed, a learner could "correct" a correct indicative or misread *siempre que* + indicative as a conditional. The page should say: subjunctive = "provided that", indicative = "whenever".
- **Source**: B&B §20.4.8 and §20.5.6 note (1) ("Siempre que… may also mean 'whenever'").

## es-b2-correlative-comparison
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "The correct frame is always cuanto más… más…"
- **Problem**: *Mientras más… más…* is very common and accepted in Latin America, and *entre más* is considered correct in Mexico and Central America. "Always" teaches learners to reject a standard regional variant. *Cuanto más* is the safe pan-Hispanic choice but not the only correct one.
- **Source**: B&B §6.11 note (1).

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "The phrase *más y más does not exist in standard Spanish."
- **Problem**: *Más y más* is attested and grammatical (*la quería más y más*), though *cada vez más* is the usual and more idiomatic equivalent. The page should say "less idiomatic", not "does not exist" with an asterisk.
- **Source**: B&B §6.12 (*cada vez más* is "the usual" equivalent, not the only one); expert knowledge.

## es-b2-past-subjunctive
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it — "Spanish has two interchangeable forms: -ara and -iera (or -ase and -iese in some regions, though the former pair is more common)."
- **Problem**: *-ara* and *-iera* are not two interchangeable forms. They are the same -ra form for -ar verbs vs. -er/-ir verbs. The two interchangeable forms are the **-ra** set (*hablara/comiera*) and the **-se** set (*hablase/comiese*). The -se set is also not regional: it is used across the Spanish-speaking world, more often in Spain and in writing. The formation callout states this correctly, so the page contradicts itself.
- **Source**: B&B ch.16 (imperfect subjunctive -ra / -se forms); internal contradiction.

## es-b2-perception-verbs
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: what is it — "ver/oír que + finite clause, is always available as a neutral alternative"; when to use it — "This option is aspectually neutral and is always grammatically correct"
- **Problem**: *Ver que* / *oír que* + finite clause is not a neutral paraphrase of direct perception. *Oír que…* normally means "to hear (be told) that…" (hearsay), and *ver que…* often means "to realize or notice that…". *Oí que los vecinos discutían* suggests "I heard that the neighbours were arguing", not "I heard them arguing". Presenting it as an all-purpose substitute will produce the wrong meaning.
- **Source**: expert knowledge.

## es-b2-quantifiers-advanced
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "The connector que is correct only when introducing a comparison target that is a clause or pronoun: gana el doble que yo"
- **Problem**: *Que* introduces any comparison target, including a full noun phrase: *gana el doble que su hermano*, *tiene el doble de habitantes que la nuestra* (from the comparatives page). Following "only a clause or pronoun", a learner would produce *gana el doble de su hermano*. The real contrast is: *de* + the quantity noun being multiplied, *que* + the entity compared against.
- **Source**: expert knowledge; B&B ch.6 (comparison).

  The page's own first pitfall also admits the imperfect indicative after *si*, so the page contradicts itself.

## es-b2-reported-speech-backshift
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "*me pidió que la ayude mixes a past main verb with a present subjunctive — a tense-sequence error… a past main verb be followed by an imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive, never a present subjunctive."
- **Problem**: "Never" is wrong. A past reporting verb + present subjunctive is accepted when the requested action has still not taken place at the moment of speaking (*El secretario pidió ayer a EE. UU. que no actúe unilateralmente*). It is very common in the media and normal in Latin-American usage. The imperfect subjunctive is the safe default, but the asterisk is unjustified.
- **Source**: B&B §20.8 (e) ("there are no rigid rules of tense agreement…"; present subjunctive "common, especially in the media") and note (4).

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "Leaving the preterite unchanged — dijo que llegó — is common in informal speech but is considered imprecise in writing and formal registers"
- **Problem**: Keeping the preterite in reported speech is fully standard in Spanish when the time of the event is clear (*Dijo que llegó ayer a las ocho*, *Me contó que lo compró en Madrid*). The backshift to the pluperfect is optional, not a marker of careful style, so learners should not "correct" a correct preterite.
- **Source**: expert knowledge.

## es-b2-sino-adversatives
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "Pero can only link two full clauses, not replace a negated adjective with another adjective."
- **Problem**: *Pero* freely links adjectives, adverbs and phrases: *Es pobre pero honrado*, *despacio pero seguro*, *Habla francés, pero mal* (the page's own correct example in the third pitfall). The real point is that *pero* does not *correct* a negated element. "Only full clauses" is false.
- **Source**: expert knowledge; internal contradiction (third pitfall).

## es-b2-subjunctive-compound
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "The choice depends on the tense of the main verb, not on how far back the event occurred… Do not use the pluperfect subjunctive when the main clause is in the present."
- **Problem**: A present main clause + pluperfect subjunctive is standard when the subordinate clause is counterfactual or refers to a past that precedes another past: *No creo que lo hubiera conseguido sin ti* ("I don't think I would have managed it without you"), *Me extraña que no lo hubieran avisado antes de la reunión*. Ojalá + pluperfect, which the page itself teaches, also has no past main verb. Tense agreement is a tendency, not a rigid rule.
- **Source**: B&B §20.8 ("there are no rigid rules of tense agreement"); expert knowledge.

## es-b2-subjunctive-negated-opinion
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "Writing *no me dijo que había venido treats the clause as reported speech (which would use the indicative), rather than as a denial. The correct form is no me dijo que hubiera venido." Also the example "No me dijo que hubiera venido a la fiesta."
- **Problem**: *No me dijo que había venido* is fully grammatical and is the normal form when the speaker takes the fact as true ("he came, but he didn't tell me"). The subjunctive is used only when the content itself is in doubt or denied. Marking the indicative wrong teaches a false rule.
- **Source**: B&B §20.3.15 note (2) (indicative after negated verbs of knowing or saying when the content is a known fact, e.g. *no confesaba que había robado el dinero*).

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it — "The subjunctive is required in the subordinate clause whenever the main clause contains one of the following negated structures"; pitfalls "These expressions are categorical denials… always govern the subjunctive."
- **Problem**: Negative imperatives and negative questions take the indicative: *No creas que es fácil*, *¿No es verdad que ha dicho eso?*. These are very common, and with "whenever / always" a learner would produce *no creas que sea fácil*.
- **Source**: B&B §20.3.15 note (1), §20.3.16.

## es-b2-subjunctive-temporal-concessive
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it — "The expressions por mucho que and por más que always take the subjunctive."; example note "Por mucho que always requires the subjunctive"
- **Problem**: These take the indicative when the event is or was a reality: *Por más que se lo dije, no lo hizo*, *Por mucho que gritaba, nadie lo oía*. The subjunctive is required for future or hypothetical reference, or for emphasis. "Always" is false and contradicts the page's own fact-vs-hypothesis logic for *aunque*.
- **Source**: B&B §20.5.2 ("if the event referred to is or was a reality, the indicative may be used").
