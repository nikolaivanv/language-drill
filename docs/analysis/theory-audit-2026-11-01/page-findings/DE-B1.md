## de-b1-hin-her
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation, example + note: "Sie geht hinein. → Sie geht hin**ein**." / "As a separable prefix, `hinein` splits into `hin-` on the verb and `-ein` at the end"
- **Problem**: `hinein` does not split internally. The whole compound `hinein-` is the separable prefix of `hineingehen`: *Sie geht hinein* / *Sie ist hineingegangen* / *…, dass sie hineingeht*. There is no "hin- on the verb, -ein at the end" pattern. The "→" example contrasts two identical sentences, and the explanation makes up a morphological rule.
- **Source**: Durrell §7.2.4 (*Er ist (in das Zimmer) hineingegangen*; *Er geht in das Haus hinein*)

## de-b1-subordinate-conjunctions
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: examples in context, note on *Bevor du gehst, mach bitte das Licht aus.*: "Separable verb `ausgehen` in the main clause"
- **Problem**: The main-clause verb is `ausmachen` (*mach … aus*, "turn off"), not `ausgehen` ("go out", or a light going out by itself). A learner who copies the note will produce *\*Geh das Licht aus*.
- **Source**: expert knowledge

## de-b1-konjunktiv-ii-past
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation callout: "Pattern: hätte/wäre [… participle] … The participle is always the final element in its clause."
- **Problem**: This is false in two common cases. (1) Subordinate clauses: the page's own headline example *Wenn ich das gewusst **hätte*** ends in the auxiliary. (2) Modals: the "Regrets … should or could have done" use the page lists needs the double infinitive *hätte … machen sollen/können*, with no participle at all. A learner following the rule writes *\*wenn ich das hätte gewusst* or *\*ich hätte das gesollt machen*.
- **Source**: Durrell §15.1.2 (*Sie hätte das Buch lesen sollen*; *Das Buch, das ich hätte kaufen sollen*), §15.3.7

## de-b1-praeteritum
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation callout "Personal endings at a glance": "Weak and mixed verbs: ich –, du -st, er/sie/es –, wir -n, ihr -t, sie/Sie -n. Strong verbs use the same endings, but the 1sg/3sg have no ending"
- **Problem**: Strong verbs take **-en**, not -n, in wir/sie/Sie (*kam-en*, *ging-en*), so "the same endings" would give *\*kamn*. The "but" is also a false contrast: weak 1sg/3sg have no ending either (*machte*), as the callout itself says one line earlier. The paradigm tables are correct; the summary contradicts them.
- **Source**: expert knowledge (Durrell ch. 10, Präteritum paradigms)

## de-b1-progressive-equivalents
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it: "the colloquial `beim + nominalized infinitive`"; when to use it: "common in everyday spoken German but is considered colloquial or regional in formal writing"; example note on *Ich bin beim Kochen*: "avoid in formal or academic contexts"
- **Problem**: Durrell treats **beim** + infinitival noun as the **standard** construction. The informal or regional one is **am** + infinitival noun (*am Lesen*), which the page covers separately in its pitfalls. The page attaches the am-progressive's register warning to beim.
- **Source**: Durrell §12.5c(i) "In standard German *beim* is used…" vs §12.5c(ii) *am* "in informal registers"

## de-b1-genitive
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation: "masculine and neuter nouns add -(e)s to the noun itself"; table rows masculine/neuter "-(e)s"; pitfall: "Dropping -(e)s from masculine or neuter nouns. The article alone is not enough."
- **Problem**: This is stated as absolute, but weak masculines, which are high-frequency at B1, take **-(e)n** and no -s: *des Studenten, des Kollegen, des Menschen, des Herrn*. The *Name* group takes *-ns* (*des Namens*), and *Herz* takes *des Herzens*. Following the page gives *\*des Studentes*, *\*des Kollege(s)*. The app's own n-declension page contradicts this one.
- **Source**: Durrell §1.3.2, §1.3.3

## de-b1-articles-use
- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it: "masculine names (e.g. der Iran, der Irak) … always require the article, including after prepositions"
- **Problem**: For masculine country names the article is usual but **optional**: *(der) Iran*, *in/im Sudan*, *in Irak*. Only feminine and plural country names always take it.
- **Source**: Durrell §4.4.1a ("the definite article is usual, but optional: (der) Libanon, (der) Iran, in/im Sudan") vs §4.4.1b; also §1.1.6h

## de-b1-dass-clause-perfekt
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls: "the required order is always participle first, auxiliary last"; warn callout: "make sure the auxiliary is the absolute last word"
- **Problem**: With a modal (or *lassen*, *sehen*, *hören*) in the Perfekt, the "always … last" rule breaks: in the double-infinitive construction the auxiliary comes *before* the verb cluster, as in *…, dass sie es **hat** machen müssen*. B1 learners meet modal Perfekt in subordinate clauses, and this rule produces *\*dass sie es machen müssen hat*.
- **Source**: Durrell §15.1.2 (*Sie hat mir gesagt, dass sie es hat machen müssen*)

## de-b1-passive-werden
- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation warn callout: "In a modal passive, the verb cluster always ends with werden: modal → Partizip II → werden."
- **Problem**: This only holds in main clauses. In subordinate clauses the finite modal comes last (*…, weil das Formular ausgefüllt werden **muss***). In the Perfekt/Konjunktiv the auxiliary comes first (*…, weil der Vertrag hätte überprüft werden sollen*). "Always" teaches *\*weil es muss ausgefüllt werden*.
- **Source**: Durrell §15.1.2 (*… weil der Vertrag hätte überprüft werden sollen*), §19.1.3
