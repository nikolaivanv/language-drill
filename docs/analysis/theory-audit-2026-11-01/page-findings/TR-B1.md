## tr-b1-causative-voice

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it — "The person who is caused to act (the _causee_) is marked with the *dative case (-A)* when the base verb is intransitive, and with the *dative case (-A)* when the base verb is transitive"; and common pitfalls — "«I made Ali run» is Ali'ye koşturdum, not *Ali'yi koşturdum."
- **Problem**: The page reverses the rule for intransitive bases. When the base verb is intransitive, the causee goes in the **accusative**: *Ali'yi koşturdum*, *Adamı öldürdüler*. The dative is for the causee of a transitive base: *Deniz'e kapıyı açtırdı*. The page's own examples (*Çocuğu uyuttum*, *Onu güldürdün*) follow the correct rule, so they contradict the prose. The pitfall marks the correct form as wrong.
- **Source**: G&K §13.2.1 (i) for the dative with transitive bases, (ii) for the accusative with intransitive bases, ex. (33)–(34)

- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation — "[-t] — used after *polysyllabic stems* and after any stem ending in a *vowel, -l, or -r*. Examples: uyu → uyut-, kayna → kaynat-, kapat → kapat-."; and pitfall — "Stems ending in a vowel, -l, or -r, and all polysyllabic stems, take [-t], not [-DIr]."
- **Problem**: -t is used only on **polysyllabic stems ending in a vowel, l or r**. Monosyllabic stems ending in l or r take -DIr (*öl→öldür*, *gül→güldür*, *gör→gördür*), and the page's own table shows these. Polysyllabic stems ending in another consonant also take -DIr (*konuş→konuştur*, *değiş→değiştir*). The example *kapat → kapat-* is wrong too: *kapat-* is already the causative of *kapa-*, and its causative is *kapattır-*.

## tr-b1-abstract-postpositions

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation table — "onların | hakkında / hakklarında | about them"
- **Problem**: *hakklarında* is misspelled. Gemination appears only before a vowel, so the correct form is *haklarında*. The standard form is also not genitive *onların* but non-case-marked *onlar hakkında*. Plural pronouns are not genitive-attracting, and a 3pl complement takes the 3sg possessive on the postposition.
- **Source**: G&K §17.3.2 (iv)–(v), ex. (85); §17.2.1 (plural pronouns stay unmarked)

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: what is it? — "Unlike Group-1 postpositions (e.g. _ile, için_), these forms are historically frozen noun + locative/ablative constructions"
- **Problem**: This misuses the terminology. In G&K, "Group 1" is the **spatial possessive-marked** postpositions (*önünde*, *arkasında*). *ile* and *için* are bare postpositions. The case description is also wrong: *yerine* is dative and *boyunca* has -CA, so the forms are not all locative/ablative.
- **Source**: G&K §17.2 vs §17.3.1 / §17.3.2

## tr-b1-converb-while-yken

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it — "**Imperfective aspect only** — -ken describes an ongoing or habitual state, so it attaches to imperfective bases (aorist [-r/-ar/-er], present continuous [-iyor]), never to a completed-past (-DI) stem."
- **Problem**: -(y)ken is tense/aspect-neutral and attaches to other position-3 markers: **-mIş** (*göstermişken*, "having done") and **-(y)AcAk** (*gelecekken*, "although they were going to come"), as well as -mAz and -mAlI. The ban on -DI is correct, but "imperfective only" is false.
- **Source**: G&K §26.2.3 (ii), ex. (42)–(43); §8.5.2.2

- **Problem**: *bit-* is intransitive ("to end, be finished"). "We need to finish" is *bitirmemiz gerekiyor*, which is what the page's own example uses. *bitmemiz gerekiyor* means roughly "we need to come to an end".

## tr-b1-olarak

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "**Incorrect:** _avukatı olarak çalışıyor_. **Correct:** _avukat olarak çalışıyor_. The complement is always bare."; also formation — "The complement takes no case suffix."
- **Problem**: *avukatı* here is ambiguous, and in its most natural reading it is the 3sg **possessive** ("as his/her lawyer"), not the accusative. *(Şirketin) avukatı olarak çalışıyor* and *Türkiye'nin başkenti olarak* are fully grammatical. "No case suffix" is true, but "always bare" is false: possessive-marked complements are normal. The pitfall marks a correct sentence as wrong.
- **Source**: expert knowledge

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: examples, note on *masa olarak* — "Compare: _masaya benziyor_ «it looks like a table» would need _gibi_, not olarak."
- **Problem**: *masaya benziyor* is complete and correct with *benze-* + dative, and needs no *gibi*. The note states a false requirement.
- **Source**: expert knowledge

## tr-b1-participles-dik-acak

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation — "Both suffixes follow standard 4-way vowel harmony."
- **Problem**: -(y)AcAK has two-way (A-type) harmony (*-acak/-ecek*), as the next sentence on the page says. Only -DIK has four-way harmony.
- **Source**: G&K ch. 3 (A- vs I-type harmony)

## tr-b1-passive-voice

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: examples — "Burada sigara [içilmez]." note: "Impersonal passive on the intransitive verb `iç-` («to drink/smoke»). No subject exists in the sentence."; also when to use it (same example under "Impersonal passive on intransitives").
- **Problem**: *iç-* is transitive, and *sigara* is its object, which becomes the passive subject. This is an ordinary personal passive, not an impersonal passive of an intransitive verb. A real impersonal example would be *Burada koşulmaz* or *Bu yoldan gidilir*. The example teaches the wrong idea of what "impersonal passive" means.
- **Source**: G&K §13.2.2.3 ("The addition of a passive suffix to an intransitive verb produces an impersonal passive")

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "The ablative suffix -dan/-den marks source or cause, not agent. … Always use **tarafından** when naming the agent of a passive verb."
- **Problem**: G&K list four ways to express the agent: *tarafından*, a locative or ablative NP, and -CA. *tarafından* is normally restricted to animate (mostly human) agents. Inanimate agents are usually expressed with the ablative or locative (*Keten ayışığından parçalanır*, *Fırtınada bütün evler yıkıldı*), and institutional agents with -CA (*okulumuzca düzenlendi*). "Always use tarafından" is false.
- **Source**: G&K §13.2.2 (text before ex. (42), and ex. (43)–(46))

## tr-b1-real-conditional

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation — "For most verbs the aorist is formed with [-(I)r] … The buffer vowel [I] in -(I)r follows four-way vowel harmony (ı, i, u, ü) and appears only after a consonant-final stem."
- **Problem**: This is the same class of error as the aorist-stem mistake already caught on another page. Monosyllabic consonant-final stems take **-Ar** (*yap-ar*, *yağ-ar*, *iç-er*), except for the 13 -Ir verbs (*al, bil, bul, dur, gel, gör, kal, ol, öl, var, ver, vur*, and *san-*). Polysyllabic stems take -(I)r. The page's own examples (*yağarsa*, *edersek*) use -Ar, contradicting the rule. A learner following it would produce *\*yapırsa*, *\*yağırsa*.
- **Source**: G&K §8.2.3.3 (aorist -(A/I)r distribution)

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation callout — "A small set of common verbs has an irregular aorist stem. The most important at B1 are: _git-_ → [giderse], _et-_ → [ederse], _de-_ → [derse]."
- **Problem**: These are not irregular aorists. *gider/eder* are regular monosyllabic -Ar forms with the regular t→d alternation, and *der* is a regular vowel-final stem + -r. The verbs that really have to be learned are the 13 monosyllabic -Ir verbs, and the page never mentions them.
- **Source**: G&K §8.2.3.3

## tr-b1-reciprocal-voice

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "`✗ yardımlaştık` is not standard Turkish. … use «birbiri»: `✓ birbirimize yardım ettik`"
- **Problem**: *yardımlaşmak* ("to help one another") is a standard, common Turkish verb (*yardımlaşma*, *yardımlaşma derneği*), formed with denominal -lAş, which productively expresses mutual activity on nouns. The page labels a correct word as an error.
- **Source**: G&K §7.2.2.1 (-lAş on nouns "to indicate mutual activity": haberleş-, mektuplaş-); expert knowledge for yardımlaş-

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation table and callout — "selamla- | selamlaş-", "mektupla- | mektuplaş-"; "«selamlaş-» and «mektuplaş-» end in [-ş] rather than «-ış…» because the stem already ends in a vowel, so only «-ş» is added."
- **Problem**: These are **noun + -lAş** (*selam*, *mektup* + -lAş), not verb stem + reciprocal -(I)ş. There is no verb *\*mektupla-*. This misattribution also fuels the wrong *yardımlaş* pitfall above: -lAş on nouns is the live pattern.
- **Source**: G&K §7.2.2.1; §8.2.1.4

## tr-b1-reflexive-voice-kendi

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation table — "örtü- | örtün- | cover oneself"
- **Problem**: The verb stem is *ört-* ("cover"), and *örtü* is the noun "a cover". The form is *ört-ün-*: a consonant-final stem plus buffer vowel. Listing it as vowel-final *örtü-* + *-n* contradicts the page's own rule and gives a non-existent verb stem.
- **Source**: G&K §8.2.1.3 (ört- → örtün-); §13.2

## tr-b1-since-converb

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls — "The optative suffix [-(y)AlIm] (1st pl. «let's…») and the 3rd person optative [-(y)AlI] look similar to the converb."
- **Problem**: Turkish has no 3rd-person optative *-(y)AlI*. The 3rd-person form is the imperative/optative -sIn (*gitsin*), and the optative series is -(y)AyIm, -(y)AlIm, and so on. This invents a form.
- **Source**: G&K §8.3.3 / person markers on the optative (3rd person -sIn attaches directly to the verb); §21.4.4.3

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation — "The vowel in the suffix follows four-way vowel harmony (A = a/e)."
- **Problem**: -(y)AlI has two-way harmony only (*-alı/-eli*), as the "A = a/e" in the same sentence and the table (*geleli, taşınalı, okuyalı, göreli*) show. "Four-way" is false.

## tr-b1-when-converbs

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: what is it? — "[-(y)IncA] and [-DIğIndA] … They are not interchangeable."; plus the "Quick test" in when to use it
- **Problem**: G&K say -DIğIndA / -DIğI zaman are "sometimes used interchangeably with -(y)IncA". Their more *characteristic* use is the ongoing-background reading. A strict "not interchangeable" plus the "as soon as" test would make learners reject natural sentences such as *Eve geldiğimde seni ararım*. The page's own example *Onu gördüğüm zaman çok mutlu oldum* is sequential, so it contradicts the rule.
- **Source**: G&K §26.3.16 (ii)
