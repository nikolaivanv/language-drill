## de-b2-dass-equivalents

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls → "Dropping an obligatory correlate." — "✗ _Wir freuen uns, dass ihr uns besucht._ … Verbs that govern a preposition (e.g. `sich freuen auf`, `warten auf`, `achten auf`) require the matching da(r)+preposition correlate … Omitting it produces an ungrammatical sentence."
- **Problem**: *Wir freuen uns, dass ihr uns besucht* is fully grammatical. The page even uses this exact sentence as a correct example under "when to use it". For most prepositional verbs the correlate is optional. Hammer lists *sich freuen auf/über*, *achtgeben auf* and *aufpassen auf* among the verbs with which it is "often left out". Correct statement: the correlate is often optional and adds emphasis. It is obligatory only with some verbs, e.g. *sich verlassen auf*.
- **Source**: §16.5.14

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → "Modal verbs do not take [zu] and cannot head a zu-infinitive construction."
- **Problem**: This is false. Modals form zu-infinitives freely: *Ich hoffe, dich bald wiedersehen zu können*, *ohne es erklären zu können*, *um bleiben zu dürfen*. A learner who follows this rule cannot say "hope to be able to".
- **Source**: §11.2.2d (the example "Ich hoffe dich bald wiedersehen zu können"); §15 ("nicht lesen zu können")

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it → "zu-infinitive — identical subjects only" and pitfall → "The zu-infinitive is only valid when both clauses share the same subject."
- **Problem**: With verbs like *bitten, empfehlen, raten, erlauben, verbieten*, the subject of the infinitive is the *object* of the main clause: *Ich bitte dich, früher zu kommen*. Here the subjects differ, and a zu-infinitive is the normal choice. The rule as stated would push learners to *Ich bitte dich, dass du früher kommst*.
- **Source**: §11.2.3 (object of *bitten* as the infinitive's subject)

## de-b2-fixed-prepositions

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: callout in formation → "The da(r)-correlate is **required** when the complement is a clause. It cannot be omitted". Pitfall → "Saying `*Er ist stolz, dass er gewonnen hat.` omits the required correlate."
- **Problem**: Hammer says there are "no hard and fast rules" here. With many adjectives the correlate is optional, and sentences without it are "equally acceptable and grammatical". *Er ist stolz, dass …*, *Ich habe Angst, dass …* and *Ich bin froh, dass …* are all standard. Correct statement: the correlate is common, especially in writing, but often optional.
- **Source**: §6.4.1c; §16.5.14

## de-b2-indefinite-pronouns

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → "`irgendein-` declines exactly like the indefinite article `ein-` with strong endings where the article has none (nominative masculine and neuter, accusative neuter)". The table gives nom. masc. *irgendeiner* and neut. *irgendein(e)s*. Callout → "As a determiner before a noun, `irgendein` (without `-s`) is also accepted."
- **Problem**: The page introduces *irgendein-* as "used as a determiner before a noun", then gives it pronoun endings. Before a noun the determiner takes exactly the endings of *ein*: *irgendein Mann*, *irgendein Buch*. *irgendeiner Mann* and *irgendeines Buch* are wrong. The callout reverses the norm: *irgendein* is the only correct determiner form, not merely "also accepted". The strong forms *irgendeiner* and *irgendein(e)s* belong to the pronoun.
- **Source**: §5.5.11d(i)–(ii)

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: stand-alone table, column "pl. / mass noun" with nom./acc. *welche*. Example note: "`welche` is the partitive pronoun for mass nouns (feminine or neuter singular, and plurals)."
- **Problem**: *welcher* used for a mass noun agrees with that noun's gender, so masculine mass nouns take *welchen* in the accusative: *Hast du Käse? – Ja, ich habe welchen*. Grouping all mass nouns under plural *welche*, and limiting it to feminine/neuter, leads to *ich habe welche* for Käse, Kaffee or Zucker.
- **Source**: §5.5.26

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it → "**mehrere / einige** — … Both are always plural".
- **Problem**: *einig-* has singular uses with mass and abstract nouns: *vor einiger Zeit*, *mit einigem Glück*, *nach einigem Überlegen*. Neuter *einiges* is also common. Only *mehrere* is plural-only.
- **Source**: §5.5.7a

## de-b2-konjunktiv-ii

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation table, column "Präteritum stem": "können | konn-", "dürfen | durf-", "sollen | soll-", "wollen | woll-".
- **Problem**: These are not the Präteritum stems. The stems are *konnt-, durft-, sollt-, wollt-*, matching the correct *musst-* and *wusst-* in the same table. A learner who derives the subjunctive from these cells gets nonsense forms.
- **Source**: §10 (Table of modal past forms: konnte/durfte/sollte/wollte)

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation → "Synthetic forms are built from the simple past (Präteritum) stem, with an umlaut added where possible".
- **Problem**: Weak verbs never take an umlaut: the form is identical to the past indicative (*holte*, not *hölte*). *sollen* and *wollen* also keep *sollte/wollte*. The umlaut applies to most strong verbs, *haben/sein* and some modals. As written, the rule predicts *söllte* and *wöllte*.
- **Source**: §10.5.2a

## de-b2-mittelfeld-word-order

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it → "For _clausal negation_ … [nicht] stands as late as possible — after all adverbials and objects, but before any right-bracket infinitive or participle". The slot table places "10 — nicht (clausal)" after slot 8 (Mo) and slot 9 (Lo).
- **Problem**: Clausal *nicht* follows noun objects and most adverbials. It comes *before* manner adverbials and verb complements: directional or locative complements, prepositional objects and predicatives. Examples: *Er fährt nicht nach Berlin*, *Ich warte nicht auf ihn*, *Er arbeitet nicht schnell*. Following the page gives *Er fährt nach Berlin nicht* and *Ich warte auf ihn nicht*.
- **Source**: §19.6a ("just before any adverbs of manner and verb complements"; "nicht follows all adverbials except those of manner")

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls → "The noun-phrase rule (dative NP before accusative NP) only holds when at least one object is a full noun phrase."
- **Problem**: The condition should be "both objects are full noun phrases". The page's own callout says a pronoun precedes a noun phrase regardless of case. So with one pronoun the order is *Ich gebe es dem Mann*, which is accusative before dative. The pitfall as worded predicts the opposite.
- **Source**: §19.4.1–19.4.2

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation slot table: "4 — dative noun phrase", "5 — accusative noun phrase", then "6 — Te: temporal adverbial" through "9 — Lo". The table is described as "the canonical left-to-right slot order".
- **Problem**: Hammer's default is the reverse for the accusative noun object. Adverbials "typically come after the noun subject and the dative object, but before the accusative object" (*Ich habe gestern im Büro den Bericht gelesen*). A definite, given accusative object can move left, but the table presents the marked order as canonical.
- **Source**: §19.5.1 / Table 19.9

## de-b2-modal-particles-advanced

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation callout → "A particle can only appear once per clause. Stacking two modal particles in the same clause is possible … but rare and stylistically marked. At B2, treat each clause as carrying one particle."
- **Problem**: Combinations of particles are frequent and idiomatic: *doch mal*, *halt mal*, *ja wohl*, *denn eigentlich*, *ja schon*. The callout also contradicts itself ("can only appear once" vs. "stacking is possible"). Telling a B2 learner to use one particle per clause blocks very common spoken patterns.
- **Source**: §9.1.6 (*denn noch*), §9.1.22 (*doch mal*)

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: pitfall → "_Sag das nur nicht!_ is a mild caution; _Sag das bloß nicht!_ carries a strong emotional warning … Using _nur_ when you intend urgency will understate your meaning significantly". Also "_Nur_ … is more neutral; _bloß_ always carries emotional weight."
- **Problem**: Hammer says warning *nur* is especially common in negative commands, and *bloß* is only "slightly more emphatic" and more colloquial. *Sag das nur nicht!* is itself a warning, not a mild caution.
- **Source**: §9.1.5, §9.1.25

## de-b2-modal-perfect-word-order

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it → "The auxiliary hätte (or wäre for motion/state-change verbs, though rare with modals) combines with the double infinitive."
- **Problem**: Modal verbs always form their perfect tenses with *haben*, even when the dependent verb is a *sein*-verb: *Er hätte kommen sollen*, *Ich habe gehen müssen*. *wäre kommen sollen* is ungrammatical, not "rare". This invites a common learner error.
- **Source**: §15.1 (e.g. "Wir haben meinen Onkel nicht besuchen können"); expert knowledge

## de-b2-nominalization

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation table row → "verb | add suffix -heit / -keit | derived noun | always feminine (die) | frei → die Freiheit"
- **Problem**: *-heit/-keit* attach to adjectives (and some nouns), not verbs. The row's own example *frei* is an adjective. The source column should say "adjective".
- **Source**: §20.2.1

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: what is it → "adjectives and participles become nouns by taking strong adjective endings after an article (e.g. `gut → das Gute`)".
- **Problem**: Nominalized adjectives decline like ordinary adjectives. After a definite article the ending is weak (*das Gute*, *der Kranke*); strong endings appear without an article or after *ein* (*ein Kranker*, *Gutes*). The page's own example note says "_das Gute_ … takes the weak adjective ending `-e`", which contradicts the intro.
- **Source**: §6 (adjectival nouns); expert knowledge

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation → "Nominalized infinitives are always singular and always neuter. They do not have a plural form."
- **Problem**: Hammer says they are "in general" not used in the plural, but lexicalized ones are: *die Schreiben* (letters), *die Leben*, *die Essen* (meals/dinners), *die Vorhaben*. "Always" is too strong.
- **Source**: §11.4.1c, §11.4.3

## de-b2-passive-alternatives

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it → "sein + zu + infinitive — … Occasionally expresses possibility, but obligation is the primary reading". Pitfall → "When ability is intended, _sich lassen_ or a _-bar_ adjective is the more natural choice."
- **Problem**: Hammer gives *können* on equal footing with *müssen/sollen*: "Die Anträge sind im Rathaus abzuholen (= können/müssen…)". The possibility reading is very frequent, especially with negation: *Er ist nicht zu erreichen*, *Das ist kaum zu glauben*, *nicht zu übersehen*. Calling it "occasional" will make learners misread these.
- **Source**: §13.4.5a

## de-b2-relatives-advanced

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls → "✗ Wer zu spät kommt, _der_ muss draußen warten. (redundant here)". Formation → "When both are nominative, no resumptive is used."
- **Problem**: A resumptive *der* with matching cases is grammatical and common for emphasis. Compare the proverbs *Wer A sagt, der muss auch B sagen* and *Wer nicht hören will, der muss fühlen*. Hammer says the demonstrative "can be added" when cases differ, but does not forbid it when they match. Marking the sentence ✗ is a false correction.
- **Source**: §5.4.5a; expert knowledge

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: when to use it → "wo(r)- + preposition — … When the relative clause requires a preposition and the antecedent is a whole clause (or an indefinite/abstract noun), form a _wo(r)-_ compound instead of _preposition + was_".
- **Problem**: With a noun antecedent, abstract or not, standard German uses preposition + *der/die/das*: *das Problem, über das wir sprachen*. Hammer says *wo(r)-* with noun antecedents "is now rare even in formal writing". *wo(r)-* belongs after *was*-type antecedents (*alles, etwas, nichts, das*, superlatives) and after clauses.
- **Source**: §5.4.4b(i)–(ii)

## de-b2-subjective-modals

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation table row → "will | unverified self-claim | Er will alles wissen."
- **Problem**: *Er will alles wissen* naturally means "He wants to know everything", the ordinary volitional use, so the example doesn't illustrate the claim reading. Epistemic *wollen* with present reference needs a context where volition is ruled out, e.g. *Er will Arzt sein* or *Er will davon nichts wissen*. It is clearest with a perfect infinitive (*will nichts gesehen haben*).
- **Source**: expert knowledge; §15 (wollen, claim sense)

## de-b2-text-reference-words

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Location**: formation table row → "solch- (attributive, after def. article, nom. sg. masc.) | der solche Fehler | — (rare; prefer: ein solcher Fehler)". Prose → "take standard adjective endings after a definite article (weak declension)".
- **Problem**: *solch-* is not used after the definite article, so *der solche Fehler* is ungrammatical rather than rare. It takes adjective endings after *ein* or quantifying determiners (*ein solcher*, *jeder solche*, *alle solchen*).
- **Source**: §5.1.6

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: formation → "The sentence-anaphor [das / dies] has no inflection in this function — it always appears in its base form regardless of the grammatical role it fills in the new sentence."
- **Problem**: The sentence-anaphoric demonstrative does take case when the role requires it: *Dem stimme ich zu*, *Dem ist nicht so*, *Dem widerspreche ich*, all dative. With a preposition it usually becomes a *da(r)-* compound. Learners following this rule would write *\*Das stimme ich zu*.

## de-b2-verb-prefixes

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: when to use it → "the prefix is **separable** when the meaning is concrete or directional … it is **inseparable** when the meaning is figurative or abstract". Callout → "**um-FAH-ren** (stress on stem) = inseparable, figurative (drive around)".
- **Problem**: For *um-* the distinction is turning or changing state (separable) vs. encirclement (inseparable), not literal vs. figurative. Inseparable *umfahren, umarmen, umsegeln, umgeben* are all literal. The page's own example note on *die Baustelle umfahren* calls it "figurative/directional", which contradicts itself. A literal/figurative rule leads learners to treat *umfahren* "drive around" as separable.
- **Source**: §20.7.6 (um-), §20.7.1 (durch-, several literal inseparable verbs)

## de-b2-word-formation

- **Severity**: error
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: common pitfalls → "`freundlich` (friendly, of behaviour) vs. `freundig` (joyful, enthusiastic — used mainly in compounds such as _kinderfreudig_)".
- **Problem**: *freundig* is not a German word. "Joyful" is *freudig*, from *Freude*, and is unrelated to *freundlich*. The example *kinderfreudig* doesn't contain "freundig" either (the related word is *kinderfreundlich*). The supposed -lich/-ig pair is invented.
- **Source**: expert knowledge

- **Severity**: misleading
- **Confidence**: high
- **Location**: pitfall → "The rule is straightforward: if the adjective already ends in `-lich` or `-ig`, use `-keit` … Reserve `-heit` for adjectives that do not end in those suffixes". The table also gives "-heit | adjective (not in -lich/-ig)".
- **Problem**: *-keit* is also used after *-bar, -sam*, and most *-el/-er* adjectives: *Dankbarkeit, Einsamkeit, Eitelkeit, Sauberkeit*. *-igkeit* is used after *-haft/-los*: *Arbeitslosigkeit*. The stated rule produces *\*Dankbarheit*, *\*Einsamheit* and *\*Sauberheit*. Hammer also notes the choice "is not wholly regular".
- **Source**: §20.2.1
